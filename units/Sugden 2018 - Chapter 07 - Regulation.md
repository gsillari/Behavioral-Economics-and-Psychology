# Sugden 2018 - Chapter 07 - Regulation

## Summary

This chapter applies Sugden's broader framework to regulation. Its central message is that regulation should not be judged mainly by whether it corrects people's supposed mistakes relative to latent preferences, but by whether it expands the conditions for mutually beneficial interaction while respecting citizens as agents.

In the current vault, this chapter matters because it shows that the book is not only abstract philosophy. It bears directly on the evaluation of paternalist regulation.

## Key claims

The first claim is that neoclassical arguments for regulation are too narrow, but planner-centered behavioral arguments can overreach in the opposite direction.

The second claim is that regulation should be assessed by its effects on opportunity and mutually advantageous cooperation, not only by corrected welfare scores.

The third claim is that the most defensible behavioral critique of policy is comparative and institutional rather than simply paternalist.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it connects the abstract dispute over welfare standards to actual regulatory reasoning. It turns a philosophical disagreement into a policy-design disagreement.

## Open questions

Can opportunity-based regulation give enough guidance in domains where harms are diffuse or long-run? And when behavioral frictions are serious, how much deference should regulation still give to actual choice?
