# Savage 1954 - Chapter 07 - Partition Problems

## Summary

This chapter develops the special class of observational problems Savage calls partition problems. It examines the value of observation, sufficient statistics, likelihood ratios, repeated observations, sequential probability ratio procedures, and standard forms for comparing observations.

In the current vault, this chapter matters because it shows how the Savage benchmark extends into statistics proper without abandoning its personalistic core.

## Key claims

The first claim is that many statistical decision problems can be represented as partition problems in which consequences depend on which member of a partition of the state space obtains.

The second claim is that the value of observation can be studied comparatively, not only locally.

The third claim is that sufficient statistics and likelihood structure can be analyzed inside a decision-theoretic framework rather than treated as external frequentist tools.

The fourth claim is that repeated observation and sequential procedures belong to the same informational architecture.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it shows that the Savage benchmark does not stop at abstract choice theory. It reaches into the methodology of statistics and evidence, which is why later critiques of sharp probabilities and benchmark-rational updating have such wide scope.
