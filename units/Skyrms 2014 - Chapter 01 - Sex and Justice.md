# Skyrms 2014 - Chapter 01 - Sex and Justice

## Place in the work

Chapter 1 establishes the book's core pattern: compare a rational-choice treatment of a social-contract problem with an evolutionary treatment, then show that the evolutionary dynamics can select or destabilize outcomes in ways the static equilibrium analysis misses.

## Summary

Skyrms begins with sex ratios because they provide a biological model for strategic evolutionary explanation. Arbuthnot had treated the near equality of male and female births as evidence of providential design. Darwin and Fisher instead show how sex ratios can be explained through differential reproductive success: when one sex is scarce, producing that sex yields higher expected reproductive returns, and the population is pushed back toward equality or equal parental investment.

Skyrms then transfers this logic to a simple bargaining game in which two agents divide a cake. Ordinary Nash equilibrium does not uniquely select equal division, because any pair of positive claims summing to the whole cake is a strict equilibrium. Rawlsian or Harsanyian veil-of-ignorance reasoning helps only if supplemented with additional decision rules. Evolutionary dynamics, by contrast, puts strategies behind what Skyrms calls a Darwinian veil of ignorance: individual identities recede and strategies compete by average success.

In the simplest model, demanding half is the unique evolutionarily stable pure strategy. But the result is not automatic. The population can fall into stable polymorphic traps, such as a mix of greedy and modest demanders. Correlation among like strategies, local interaction, finer divisibility of the good, and random variation can shrink or eliminate these traps and make approximate fair division more likely.

## Key claims

Rational self-interest alone underselects justice in the division game. It identifies many equilibria, while the fairness intuition picks one.

Evolutionary dynamics can give equal division a stronger explanatory status because a population of half-demanders resists invasion by strategies that demand more or less.

The evolutionary argument is conditional, not triumphalist. Polymorphic traps show that unfair or inefficient population states can be dynamically stable.

Positive correlation among like strategies improves the prospects for fair division by making greedy/modest polymorphisms less stable and making the fair strategy a stronger attractor.

## Evidence or method

The chapter uses formal evolutionary-game reasoning rather than laboratory evidence as its main method. It compares sex-ratio dynamics, Nash equilibrium, evolutionary stability, replicator dynamics, basin-of-attraction reasoning, and correlated interaction.

The chapter also uses the Nydegger and Owen dollar-division experiment as a simple behavioral reference point: people tend to choose equal division in the symmetric case, which gives the formal problem its explanatory target.

## Concepts touched

The main concepts are [[Evolutionary game theory]], [[Fairness]], evolutionary stability, replicator dynamics, polymorphic equilibrium, correlation, and the Darwinian veil of ignorance.

## Relation to other units

Chapter 1 previews the correlation argument developed in [[Skyrms 2014 - Chapter 03 - Mutual Aid]]. It also sets up Chapter 2, where Skyrms turns from symmetric division to the asymmetric [[Ultimatum game]] and the problem of costly punishment.

## Why it matters for PPE

This chapter gives PPE students a clean model of how "justice" can be studied naturalistically without being reduced to either moral intuition or explicit contract. It also supplies a bridge between distributive justice, game theory, and behavioral evidence about fair division.
