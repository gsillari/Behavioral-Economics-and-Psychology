# Lichtenstein and Slovic 2006 - The Construction of Preference - An Overview (in Lichtenstein and Slovic 2006, ch. 1)

## Summary

This chapter is the architectural overview of the construction-of-preference program. It argues that one of the most important lessons of behavioral decision research is that preferences are often formed or stabilized in the process of elicitation rather than simply read off from a pre-existing internal ranking.

Its main role in the current vault is to give the whole branch a proper conceptual frame, placing [[Preference reversals]] and [[Procedure invariance]] at the center of a broader challenge to revealed-preference reasoning.

## Key claims

The first claim is that preference expression is sensitive to response mode, context, framing, and task structure.

The second claim is that classic reversals are not isolated curiosities. They are signs of a deeper problem for theories that assume stable underlying preference.

The third claim is that the implications reach beyond laboratory puzzles into welfare analysis, valuation, and public policy.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it explains why preference construction is not a side note in behavioral economics. It is a direct challenge to how economics infers value from expressed choice.

## Open questions

How widely should the construction-of-preference framework be applied outside laboratory valuation tasks? And when should context sensitivity be treated as error rather than as part of what preference is?
