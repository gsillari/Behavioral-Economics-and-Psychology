# Savage 1954 - Chapter 03 - Personal Probability

## Summary

This chapter gives the book's clearest positive account of personal probability. Savage rejects purely verbal interrogation of belief and instead builds probability behaviorally out of preferences over acts. He develops qualitative and quantitative personal probability, conditional probability, and the role of experience and symmetry in learning.

In the current vault, this chapter matters because it makes subjective probability part of a decision architecture rather than a free-floating coherence idea.

## Key claims

The first claim is that probabilities to a person should be read through behavior and choice, not only through verbal reports about what seems likely.

The second claim is that personal probability can be developed both qualitatively and quantitatively inside a theory of consistent decision.

The third claim is that conditional probability and learning from experience belong to the same framework, so belief updating is not an external add-on.

The fourth claim is that symmetry can be useful, but it does not by itself define the personalistic view.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it sharpens the benchmark later challenged by [[Ellsberg 1961]]. The issue is not only whether agents have beliefs, but whether uncertain acts can be represented through one coherent personal probability structure that is decision-ready.
