# Slovic and Lichtenstein 2006 - Relative Importance of Probabilities and Payoffs in Risk Taking (in Lichtenstein and Slovic 2006, ch. 2)

## Summary

This chapter is an early empirical anchor for the preference-reversal branch. It shows that people weight probabilities and payoffs differently depending on the task they are performing, helping explain why the same risky options can be ranked differently across elicitation procedures.

Its role in the current vault is to give the branch a deeper empirical footing than the later overview chapters alone provide.

## Key claims

The first claim is that risky choice is not governed by a single stable weighting of outcomes and probabilities across tasks.

The second claim is that valuation and choice can emphasize different features of the same gamble.

The third claim is that this differential weighting helps explain later findings on [[Preference reversals]].

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it shows how a seemingly technical result about gambling choices opens into a larger challenge to stable preference and rational elicitation.

## Open questions

Are these task differences best interpreted as irrationality, as context-sensitive weighting, or as evidence that risky preference is itself partly constructed?
