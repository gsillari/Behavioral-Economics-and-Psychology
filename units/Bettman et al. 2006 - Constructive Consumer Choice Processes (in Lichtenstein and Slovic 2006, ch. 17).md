# Bettman et al. 2006 - Constructive Consumer Choice Processes (in Lichtenstein and Slovic 2006, ch. 17)

## Summary

This chapter extends the preference-construction program into consumer choice more generally. Bettman, Luce, and Payne argue that many consumer decisions are constructed through adaptive processes that respond to task complexity, time pressure, goals, and the structure of the choice environment.

Its importance in the current vault is that it broadens the branch beyond classic gambling reversals and shows why preference construction matters for ordinary market behavior.

## Key claims

The first claim is that consumers often construct preferences adaptively rather than consult a fixed global ranking.

The second claim is that strategy choice depends on features of the task, including complexity and cognitive cost.

The third claim is that this broader process perspective connects laboratory anomalies to real consumer environments, making the branch more relevant to policy and market design.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it carries the construction-of-preference branch into more ordinary economic settings. It helps explain why marketing, law, and policy cannot assume that consumers merely reveal stable tastes under different institutional arrangements.

## Open questions

How much of adaptive consumer choice is a rational response to limited resources, and how much undermines welfare inference from observed choice? When should institutions simplify choice, and when does simplification itself reshape what consumers want?
