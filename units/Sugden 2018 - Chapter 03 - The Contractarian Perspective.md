# Sugden 2018 - Chapter 03 - The Contractarian Perspective

## Summary

This chapter provides the book's positive philosophical frame. Sugden argues that economics should evaluate institutions from a contractarian standpoint centered on rules and arrangements that citizens can each endorse as mutually beneficial, rather than from a planner's attempt to maximize reconstructed welfare.

In the current vault, this chapter matters because it supplies the positive counterpart to the critique of paternalist behavioral welfare economics. Without it, the book would read as only negative.

## Key claims

The first claim is that normative economics should begin from relations among citizens, not from the preferences of an external evaluator.

The second claim is that mutually advantageous cooperation provides a more defensible basis for institutional appraisal than attempts to identify a person's true or purified good.

The third claim is that this contractarian perspective can absorb behavioral findings without treating them as a standing license for paternalist correction.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it is where the book becomes more than a critique of nudging. It offers a rival normative foundation that speaks directly to economics, political theory, and moral philosophy.
