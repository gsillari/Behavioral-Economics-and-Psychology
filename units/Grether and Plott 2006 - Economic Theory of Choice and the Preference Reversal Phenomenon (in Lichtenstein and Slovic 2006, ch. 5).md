# Grether and Plott 2006 - Economic Theory of Choice and the Preference Reversal Phenomenon (in Lichtenstein and Slovic 2006, ch. 5)

## Summary

This chapter is one of the strongest economics-facing statements of why preference reversals matter. Grether and Plott ask what economic theory should make of systematic reversals between bidding and choosing, and in doing so bring the construction-of-preference challenge directly into dialogue with formal choice theory.

In the current vault, this chapter matters because it prevents the branch from looking like a purely psychological anomaly literature.

## Key claims

The first claim is that preference reversals create a genuine problem for standard economic interpretations of stable preference.

The second claim is that the phenomenon cannot be dismissed easily as trivial noise if it appears systematically across elicitation modes.

The third claim is that economic theory has to confront the possibility that procedure helps shape expressed preference.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it shows that the challenge to preference stability is not just philosophical or experimental. It enters economic theory itself.
