# Moscati 2022 - History of Utility Theory (in Heilmann and Reiss 2022, ch. 2)

## Summary

This chapter reconstructs the history of utility theory from the marginal revolution through ordinalism, revealed preference, and expected utility. Its main value in the current vault is that it turns several pages that were already present into one historical sequence rather than a loose set of benchmarks.

## Key claims

The first claim is that utility theory did not develop in one straight line. Early marginalism used utility as a theory of value and choice, but later economics questioned whether utility could be measured in any direct psychological sense.

The second claim is that [[Ordinal utility theory]] was not just a technical refinement. It was a major reinterpretation of what utility talk was allowed to mean.

The third claim is that [[Revealed preference theory]] was an even sharper methodological move. It tried to make observed choice rather than introspective utility do the theoretical work.

The fourth claim is that [[Expected utility theory]] later reintroduced a cardinal utility representation in a new and narrower sense, now tied to risk rather than to direct psychic measurement.

## Why it matters for the vault

This chapter matters because it lets the vault distinguish more clearly between three things that are often blurred together:

- utility as a broad historical family of theories
- ordinal utility as a consumer-theory settlement
- expected utility as a later formal theory of risky choice

That distinction helps the rationality branch, the preference branch, and the risk branch all at once.
