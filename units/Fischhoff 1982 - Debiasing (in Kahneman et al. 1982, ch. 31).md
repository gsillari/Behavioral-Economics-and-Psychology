# Fischhoff 1982 - Debiasing (in Kahneman et al. 1982, ch. 31)

## Summary

This chapter asks the natural second question after a bias is identified: under what conditions can it be reduced? Fischhoff treats debiasing as a kind of controlled destructive testing for judgmental phenomena. If a bias disappears under certain informational, procedural, or educational conditions, that helps clarify both the bias and the prospects for improving judgment.

In the current vault, this chapter matters because it gives the corrective side of behavioral economics a proper early anchor. The literature here should not read as though the field only diagnoses failure. This chapter makes clear that part of the program was always about improving judgment, not only cataloguing its distortions.

## Key claims

The first claim is that debiasing is not merely an applied add-on. It is also a research strategy for understanding the scope and mechanism of a bias.

The second claim is that many judgmental errors are resistant to simple warning or exhortation. Effective correction may require better representations of the task, different elicitation formats, training, external aids, or redesigned environments.

The third claim is that the success or failure of a debiasing effort reveals where the burden lies: in the individual judge, in the task, or in the mismatch between the two.

## Evidence and methods

The chapter is conceptual and review-oriented. Its main value in the vault is that it organizes debiasing as a program of intervention, diagnosis, and mechanism-testing rather than as a miscellaneous set of tricks.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it shows behavioral economics moving from criticism to design. It matters for economics because institutions are often interested less in whether people are biased than in whether those biases can be mitigated. It matters for philosophy because it raises a deeper issue about improvement: do better judgments come from better people, better tools, or better environments? It matters for politics because many behavioral interventions, disclosures, and procedures can be understood as attempts to debias rather than to manipulate.

## Open questions

Which forms of debiasing genuinely improve judgment quality, and which mainly change how confidence is expressed? When is debiasing a matter of education, and when is it a matter of institutional redesign? And how should this classic chapter be balanced against ecological cautions that some apparent biases may be locally adaptive?
