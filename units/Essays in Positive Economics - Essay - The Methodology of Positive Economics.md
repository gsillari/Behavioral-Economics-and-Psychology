# Essays in Positive Economics - Essay - The Methodology of Positive Economics

## Summary

This essay is the canonical Friedman statement of economics as a positive science. Its central methodological claim is that theories should be judged primarily by the quality of their predictions rather than by whether their assumptions describe reality in psychologically or institutionally realistic detail. In the current vault, the essay matters as a methodological benchmark and foil: it is one of the clearest statements of the style of economic theorizing that later behavioral economics often resists, complicates, or qualifies.

The essay is not itself a behavioral text, and it should not dominate the wiki. But it is important for understanding why behavioral economists so often frame their work as a critique of unrealistic assumptions, as a search for descriptively better models, or as a dispute over what counts as explanatory success in economics.

## Key claims

The first claim is that positive economics should be kept analytically distinct from normative economics. The job of positive economics is to generate reliable predictions about what will happen under specified conditions, not to decide what should be done.

The second claim is that the realism of assumptions is not the main criterion for evaluating a theory. Friedman argues that good theories often use highly simplified or unrealistic assumptions, and that the relevant test is whether the theory yields sufficiently accurate predictions across a significant domain.

The third claim is that economics can be objective in roughly the same sense as the physical sciences, even though its subject matter is human action and social institutions. This is part of what made the essay so influential in the self-understanding of mainstream economics.

## Evidence and methods

This unit is methodological and argumentative rather than empirical. Friedman relies on conceptual distinctions, analogies to other sciences, and examples about theoretical idealization rather than on new data. That is exactly why it matters for the current vault: it articulates a background standard that later behavioral work often contests not by direct refutation of the essay itself, but by changing what economists expect from models of judgment and choice.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this essay matters because it makes explicit one of the deepest background disputes behind behavioral economics. It matters for economics because it offers a compact defense of predictive instrumentalism. It matters for philosophy because it raises classic questions about realism, explanation, idealization, and the status of social science. It matters for politics because policy arguments often depend on whether simplified models are treated as acceptable predictive tools even when their psychology is unrealistic.
