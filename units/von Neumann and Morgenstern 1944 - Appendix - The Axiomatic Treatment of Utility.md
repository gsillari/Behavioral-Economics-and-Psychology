# von Neumann and Morgenstern 1944 - Appendix - The Axiomatic Treatment of Utility

## Summary

The appendix gives the formal derivation of numerical utility that the main text discusses qualitatively in Chapter I. It proves that the utility axioms imply the existence of a numerical representation of utility and that any two such representations are related by a positive linear transformation. In the current vault, this is the canonical source for [[Von Neumann-Morgenstern utility theory]] and therefore for the risk-side branch of [[Expected utility theory]].

The appendix is also valuable because it states its own assumptions and pressure points with unusual clarity. The representation depends on complete ordering, probabilistic mixtures of alternatives, monotonicity, an Archimedean-style condition, and a combination rule for compound chance alternatives. Von Neumann and Morgenstern also explicitly note that completeness may be doubtful and that the combination rule may be where a specific utility or disutility of gambling would have to enter.

## Key claims

The first claim is representational. If an individual's preferences over probabilistic alternatives satisfy the axioms, then utilities can be mapped onto numbers in a way that respects preference ordering and probabilistic mixture.

The second claim is uniqueness. Utility numbers are not unique absolutely, but they are unique up to positive affine transformation. That is why VNM utility is cardinal in a specific decision-theoretic sense, not a return to unrestricted introspective utility measurement.

The third claim is that expected utility requires probability-including alternatives. The argument does not derive numerical utility from ordinary ordinal ranking alone. It derives it from preference over alternatives that can be combined with numerical probabilities.

The fourth claim is that the theory does not assume complementarity away in ordinary consumption. The appendix stresses that the probabilistic alternatives being combined are mutually exclusive imagined events, not coexisting goods. Complementarity has to be handled elsewhere in the theory, not inside the simple mixture formula.

The fifth claim is a self-conscious limitation. The appendix explicitly raises doubts about the completeness axiom and points to the compound-lottery combination rule as a likely place where the theory would have to change if agents have a special utility or disutility of gambling.

## Why this unit matters in the vault

This unit supplies the missing canonical foundation for the risk-side benchmark. Before this ingest, the vault's expected-utility architecture had a strong uncertainty-side source in [[Savage 1954]], but its risk-side foundation was mostly reconstructed through later historical and philosophical sources. The VNM appendix now gives the risk-side branch its own primary-source anchor.

The appendix also helps prevent a common teaching mistake. VNM utility is not simply old cardinal utility brought back. It is a numerical representation justified by preferences over risky prospects. That is why it belongs between [[Ordinal utility theory]] and later decision-theoretic models, rather than outside the history of utility theory.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this appendix matters because it clarifies what many behavioral findings are pushing against. [[Allais paradox]] challenges expected utility under risk; [[Prospect Theory]] replaces parts of the same architecture with reference dependence, loss aversion, and decision weights. Those later moves make more sense once the original VNM representation is visible.

It also matters philosophically because the appendix distinguishes measurement from interpretation. Utility can be numerically represented under certain axioms, but that does not by itself show that utility is a psychological substance, a welfare measure, or an all-purpose measure of satisfaction. The normative and descriptive status of the representation remains a separate question.
