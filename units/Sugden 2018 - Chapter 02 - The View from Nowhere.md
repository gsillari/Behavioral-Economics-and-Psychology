# Sugden 2018 - Chapter 02 - The View from Nowhere

## Summary

This chapter criticizes the aspiration to evaluate institutions from an allegedly neutral, impersonal, planner-like standpoint. Sugden argues that much welfare economics and much behavioral public policy quietly adopts such a standpoint when it claims to know what would really be best for choosers.

Its role in the current vault is mainly philosophical. It prepares the move into the contractarian perspective by attacking the supposed neutrality of benevolent-planner reasoning.

## Key claims

The first claim is that the "view from nowhere" is not actually normatively neutral. It imports substantive judgments about whose standpoint counts and what should be optimized.

The second claim is that policy arguments become misleading when they present these judgments as if they were merely technical implications of behavioral evidence.

The third claim is that a more defensible normative basis must begin from relations among citizens rather than from a detached evaluator standing outside those relations.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it sharpens the philosophical stakes of behavioral public policy. The question is not only whether interventions work, but who is entitled to define improvement.
