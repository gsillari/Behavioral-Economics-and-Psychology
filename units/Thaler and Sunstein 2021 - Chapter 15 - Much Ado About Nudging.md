# Thaler and Sunstein 2021 - Chapter 15 - Much Ado About Nudging

## Summary

This chapter is the book's direct response to criticism. It revisits objections from across the political spectrum and defends nudging as choice-preserving steering rather than covert coercion. In the final edition, it is especially useful because it clarifies how the authors understand the costs of opt-out, the importance of transparency, and the difference between helpful nudges and burdensome sludge.

## Key claims

The first claim is that many objections to nudging exaggerate the contrast between steering and noninterference, because some architecture is always present.

The second claim is that the best nudges preserve easy exit and should be judged partly by the burdens they impose on those who want to choose differently.

The third claim is that criticism of nudging is often strongest where opt-out is difficult, where architecture is opaque, or where institutions use friction strategically.

The fourth claim is that nudges should be evaluated comparatively rather than rhetorically: against realistic alternatives, including existing bad architectures.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it is where the behavioral-policy argument becomes openly philosophical and political. It forces the question of whether preserved formal choice is enough, how transparency matters, and which criticisms genuinely challenge the framework rather than merely react to its label.
