# Lewis 1969 - Chapter 02 - Convention Refined

## Summary

Chapter 2 refines the initial account of [[Convention]]. Its central move is to make the expectation structure explicit. A convention is not only a behavioral regularity plus individual incentives; it also depends on a shared epistemic background in which conformity, expectations of conformity, and conditional preferences are mutually known.

The key technical contribution for the vault is [[Common knowledge]]. Lewis uses common knowledge to explain why conventions are self-sustaining. People conform because they expect others to conform, expect others to expect them to conform, and so on. This recursive expectation structure gives conventions their stability.

## Key claims

The first claim is that knowledge of convention is not an optional psychological add-on. If a regularity is to solve a coordination problem, agents must know enough about others' expectations to make their own conformity rational.

The second claim is that conventions admit alternatives. A convention is conventional partly because another regularity could have played the same coordinating role if expectations had settled differently.

The third claim is that conventions can have degrees. Some regularities are stronger or weaker depending on how widespread conformity is, how secure expectations are, and how stable the conditional preferences are.

The fourth claim is that conventions have consequences beyond immediate coordination. Once a convention is established, it can organize expectations, support communication, and become part of the background structure of social life.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it supplies the epistemic machinery behind social institutions. Institutions often work not merely because people behave in a certain way, but because everyone knows enough about everyone else's expected behavior for the pattern to become self-enforcing.

This gives PPE a bridge from strategic interaction to norm-governed social order. It is especially important for later work on law, language, money, traffic, political convention, and institutional stability.
