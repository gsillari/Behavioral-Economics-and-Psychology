# Thaler and Sunstein 2021 - Chapter 04 - When Do We Need a Nudge?

## Summary

This chapter gives the book's clearest practical criterion for when nudges are most useful. The answer is not "always." Nudges are most needed where attention is scarce, choices are difficult, feedback is poor, and people struggle to translate complex situations into manageable terms. The chapter also argues that markets do not automatically fix those problems, because firms often have incentives to profit from them.

## Key claims

The first claim is that the best nudges are those most likely to help and least likely to inflict harm.

The second claim is that nudges are especially appropriate in unfamiliar, infrequent, complex, or feedback-poor decisions.

The third claim is that bounded attention and self-control problems create a policy space even where coercion is unnecessary.

The fourth claim is that markets sometimes amplify rather than reduce behavioral frailties, because sellers may cater to confusion, inertia, or temptation.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it makes the nudge program more discriminating than a generic "people are biased" story. It asks when intervention is justified, where the strongest case lies, and why ordinary market optimism may not be enough.

## Open questions

How sharply can policy makers distinguish cases where nudges help from cases where they merely substitute expert confidence for citizen judgment? When do market responses correct frailty, and when do they exploit it?
