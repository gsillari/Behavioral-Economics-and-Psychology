# Savage 1954 - Chapter 02 - Preliminary Considerations on Decision in the Face of Uncertainty

## Summary

This is the deepest architectural chapter in the book. Savage shifts the analysis of uncertainty from propositions to acts and decisions, defines the basic elements of the framework such as person, world, states, events, consequences, and acts, and introduces the sure-thing principle together with the first postulates for rational preference.

In the current vault, this chapter is essential because it shows that Savage's theory is not merely subjective probability plus expected value. It is a full theory of preference over acts under uncertainty.

## Key claims

The first claim is that the right target for a theory of uncertainty is decision, not only belief. Extending logic means connecting uncertainty to action.

The second claim is that rationality need not force complete agreement across persons with the same evidence. That blocks a simple slide into a necessary interpretation of probability.

The third claim is that the sure-thing principle is a core structural requirement. If two acts agree outside some event, preference between them should depend only on what happens inside that event.

The fourth claim is that a formal theory of states, events, consequences, and acts can carry the benchmark side of rational choice under uncertainty.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it introduces one of the most important benchmark ideas later challenged by behavioral economics. Ellsberg's critique, and more generally the ambiguity branch, is easier to understand once the sure-thing principle is visible as a substantive commitment rather than a passing axiom.
