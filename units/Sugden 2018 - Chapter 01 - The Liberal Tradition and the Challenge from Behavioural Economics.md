# Sugden 2018 - Chapter 01 - The Liberal Tradition and the Challenge from Behavioural Economics

## Summary

This chapter frames the whole book by placing behavioral economics against the liberal tradition in economics and political thought. Sugden accepts that behavioral findings create genuine pressure on standard rational-choice assumptions, but argues that the resulting policy challenge should not be answered too quickly with paternalist correction.

Its main value in the current vault is architectural. It makes clear that the dispute is not simply over whether people err, but over what those errors are supposed to authorize in law and public policy.

## Key claims

The first claim is that behavioral economics genuinely unsettles the classical image of the chooser as fully informed, fully self-controlled, and normatively transparent.

The second claim is that this descriptive challenge does not automatically settle the normative question. A behavioral critique of rational choice does not by itself vindicate expert correction.

The third claim is that the best response is to reconstruct the liberal tradition in a way that takes behavioral findings seriously without collapsing into planner-centered paternalism.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it shows how behavioral economics becomes a political-philosophical problem. It links empirical findings about actual choice to normative questions about liberty, welfare, and legitimacy.
