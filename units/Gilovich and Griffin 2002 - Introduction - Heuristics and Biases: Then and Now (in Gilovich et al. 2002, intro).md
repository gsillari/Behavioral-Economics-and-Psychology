# Gilovich and Griffin 2002 - Introduction - Heuristics and Biases: Then and Now (in Gilovich et al. 2002, intro)

## Summary

[[Gilovich and Griffin 2002 - Introduction - Heuristics and Biases: Then and Now (in Gilovich et al. 2002, intro)]] is the architectural introduction to the green book. In the current vault, its main importance is not one new empirical claim but a field-level judgment: the heuristics-and-biases program should now be read as a mature research family with extensions, reinterpretations, and applications rather than as a small set of classic 1970s demonstrations.

The introduction matters because it explicitly positions the green book as the successor to the blue book [[Kahneman et al. 1982]]. That makes it the clearest volume-specific source for saying that the field has moved from first-generation anomaly discovery into a second stage of mechanism work, theoretical branching, and applied reach.

## Key claims

The first claim is that heuristics-and-biases remained productive because it generated not just famous findings, but a durable style of explanation for intuitive judgment under uncertainty.

The second claim is that later work should not be understood only as repetition. It extends the canonical mechanisms, sharpens process interpretation, and broadens the program into more realistic domains.

The third claim is that criticism and reinterpretation are part of the same historical family. In the current vault, this matters because the green book places classic bias work, dual-process developments, ecological challenge, and practical application in one structured volume rather than leaving them as disconnected branches.

## Why it matters for the vault

This introduction is especially useful for [[Heuristics and Biases]]. It supports the claim that the program has two foundational collective works: the blue book for early consolidation and the green book for mature extension. It also helps the vault explain why later sources such as [[Kahneman and Frederick 2002]], [[Finucane et al. 2000]], [[Stanovich and West 2000]], and [[Gigerenzer et al. 2002]] belong to one continuing story rather than to separate side branches.
