# Sugden 2018 - Chapter 05 - Opportunity

## Summary

This chapter introduces the book's core positive normative idea, now tracked in the vault as [[Opportunity criterion]]. Sugden argues that institutions should be judged by the opportunities they create for individuals to engage in mutually beneficial interaction, rather than by how well they maximize reconstructed welfare for a chooser's supposed inner rational self.

That makes this chapter the book's main positive alternative to behavioral welfare economics.

## Key claims

The first claim is that opportunity, not welfare correction, should be the central normative benchmark.

The second claim is that this benchmark can respect behavioral realism without appealing to latent preferences hidden beneath actual choices.

The third claim is that market institutions deserve defense when they expand the space of voluntary, mutually beneficial interaction.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it offers one of the clearest rival answers to the question, "If revealed preference is unstable, what should replace it?" Sugden's answer is not stronger paternalism, but an opportunity-based criterion.

## Open questions

How far can opportunity really function as a public-policy standard without reintroducing hidden welfare judgments? And what counts as a sufficient expansion of opportunity in settings marked by inequality or manipulation?
