# Angner and Loewenstein 2012 - Behavioral Economics (in Maki 2012, ch. 22)

## Summary

This chapter is a philosophy-facing overview of [[Behavioral Economics]] written from inside the field rather than from its margins. Its value in the current vault is not that it introduces a single new concept, but that it explains what behavioral economics is, how it differs from standard economics, what kinds of explanatory moves it makes, and why it matters for policy.

## Key claims

The first claim is that behavioral economics is not just a list of biases. It is a research program that modifies economic models by introducing psychologically realistic assumptions when doing so improves explanation or prediction.

The second claim is that the field operates by relaxing specific assumptions of standard economics rather than by abandoning economic analysis altogether. That keeps behavioral economics economically recognizable even when it is psychologically revisionist.

The third claim is that behavioral economics matters not only descriptively but institutionally. Once behavioral findings affect prediction, welfare analysis, and intervention design, they become relevant to law, public policy, and applied economics more broadly.

The fourth claim is that the field has to be understood at once historically, methodologically, and substantively. That is why the chapter belongs especially well near later field-history and synthesis sources in the vault.

## Why it matters for the vault

This unit matters because it gives the field a strong philosophy-of-economics presentation from authors who are already central to the vault's understanding of behavioral economics. It works especially well as a bridge between [[Camerer and Loewenstein 2004]], which maps the field as a mature research program, [[Sent 2004]], which reconstructs its professional success, and [[Angner 2019]], which argues that the later synthesis is behaviorally rather than neoclassically framed.
