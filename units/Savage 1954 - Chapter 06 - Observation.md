# Savage 1954 - Chapter 06 - Observation

## Summary

This chapter turns from static acts to informational structure. Savage analyzes observations as devices that generate derived acts, develops the value of information inside the expected-utility framework, and connects observation to dominance, admissibility, and the design of experiments.

Its importance in the current vault is that it shows how evidence enters the Savage benchmark. The theory is not only about beliefs and utilities in the abstract. It is also about what changes when agents observe something and then choose.

## Key claims

The first claim is that an observation should be modeled through the derived acts it enables, not merely through its verbal description.

The second claim is that cost-free information cannot be disadvantageous, because the agent can always ignore it if it adds no value.

The third claim is that the value of a set of acts can be analyzed inside the same expected-utility framework used for ordinary choice.

The fourth claim is that observation connects directly to admissibility and experiment design, so the transition from decision theory to statistics proper runs through informational structure.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it links the benchmark theory to questions about information, evidence, and experiment design. It is one of the reasons the Savage framework is broader than a simple probability-plus-utility schema.
