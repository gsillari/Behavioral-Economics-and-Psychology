# Savage 1954 - Chapter 05 - Utility

## Summary

This chapter constructs utility inside the broader theory of decision under uncertainty. Savage moves from gambles and their probability distributions to utility over more general acts, then introduces the idea of small worlds as a way of managing the scope of the theory.

In the current vault, this chapter matters because it shows that Savage is one of the major twentieth-century reconstructions of utility, alongside von Neumann-Morgenstern, not merely a side note to subjective probability.

## Key claims

The first claim is that preference among gambles depends only on the probability distribution of consequences, not on irrelevant descriptions of how the gamble is presented.

The second claim is that utility can be extended from simple gambles to much broader classes of acts under uncertainty.

The third claim is that utility is unique only up to the familiar affine transformations, so the representation is strong but not overdetermined.

The fourth claim is that small worlds are indispensable because rational decision theory cannot directly model every detail of the full world at once.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it deepens the history of [[Utility theory]]. Behavioral economics does not criticize a single timeless utility idea. It criticizes one branch of a larger tradition in which Savage is a major benchmark architect.
