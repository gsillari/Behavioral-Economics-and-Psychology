# Stefansson 2022 - The Economics and Philosophy of Risk (in Heilmann and Reiss 2022, ch. 3)

## Summary

This chapter gives a compact philosophy-facing map of economic theories of risk. In the current vault, its main role is to clarify how [[Expected utility theory]] models risky choice, how [[Risk attitudes]] are represented through the curvature of utility functions, and why ambiguity and nonexpected-utility criticism matter.

## Key claims

The first claim is that economics needs to distinguish certainty, risk, uncertainty, and unawareness rather than treating all noncertainty as one thing.

The second claim is that standard expected-utility theory represents risk attitudes through utility over outcomes. Concavity, convexity, and linearity are therefore not side issues. They are the formal way the theory encodes risk aversion, risk seeking, and risk neutrality.

The third claim is that this benchmark has both formal and interpretive pressure points. The chapter uses the history from Friedman-Savage to Rabin's calibration critique to show that simple diminishing marginal utility does not settle the empirical story. The key point of the Rabin-style calibration objection is that if ordinary small-stakes risk aversion were explained only by global concavity of utility over wealth, the implied curvature would often predict absurdly extreme aversion to large-stakes gambles as well. In other words, the expected-utility machinery can represent risk aversion formally, but once it is calibrated to familiar everyday choices it may generate implausible behavior elsewhere. That is why the issue is not just whether utility can be concave, but whether concavity by itself is an empirically credible explanation of observed [[Risk attitudes]].

The fourth claim is that ambiguity-sensitive behavior creates a different kind of problem from ordinary risk attitudes, because the challenge then concerns the representation of probabilities themselves.

## Why it matters for the vault

This chapter matters because it gives the risk branch a cleaner architecture. It helps keep separate:

- utility curvature over outcomes
- probability representation
- ambiguity-sensitive choice
- descriptive alternatives such as [[Prospect Theory]]
