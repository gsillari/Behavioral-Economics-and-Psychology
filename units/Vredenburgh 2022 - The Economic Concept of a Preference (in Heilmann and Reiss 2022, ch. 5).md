# Vredenburgh 2022 - The Economic Concept of a Preference (in Heilmann and Reiss 2022, ch. 5)

## Summary

This chapter reconstructs how economists use the concept of preference and why that concept has been philosophically unstable. In the current vault, its main value is that it clarifies the relation between mentalist, behaviorist, and more theory-laden interpretations of preference.

## Key claims

The first claim is that preference is not a self-interpreting term in economics. Different theories use it with different methodological commitments.

The second claim is that revealed-preference style views tried to make preference answerable to observable choice, but that move did not eliminate deeper interpretive questions.

The third claim is that the behaviorism-versus-mentalism divide is too simple on its own. Preference often functions as a theoretical term whose role depends on broader explanatory aims.

The fourth claim is that later behavioral criticism of welfare inference should therefore be read against a more complex background than a simple contrast between stable preference and behavioral anomaly.

## Why it matters for the vault

This chapter matters because it helps the preference branch stop looking like a dispute only about anomalies. It shows that the concept of preference itself has always carried unresolved methodological choices. It is now the main chapter-level anchor for [[Preference]].
