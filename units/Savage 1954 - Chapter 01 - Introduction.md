# Savage 1954 - Chapter 01 - Introduction

## Summary

This opening chapter frames the book's real target: the foundations of statistics understood through disputes about probability, decision, and rationality. Savage distinguishes objectivistic, personalistic, and necessary views of probability, explains why a strictly objectivistic view creates difficulties for statistics, and presents the book as an argument for a personalistic alternative.

Its importance in the current vault is high because it gives Savage's own map of the terrain before the formal theory begins. It also states the distinction between the deep foundational first part of the book and the later transition to statistics proper.

## Key claims

The first claim is that controversies over foundations are normal and productive rather than signs that a science has failed.

The second claim is that objectivistic interpretations of probability face a deep problem for statistical decision because they do not naturally license probabilities for propositions or expected values for acts.

The third claim is that the book will proceed in two stages: a deep first part on personalistic probability and utility, then a later transition toward statistics proper and frequentist devices.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it prevents the later Savage benchmark from looking purely technical. The formal chapters are introduced as responses to a philosophical and methodological problem: how should uncertainty, belief, and action be connected in statistics?
