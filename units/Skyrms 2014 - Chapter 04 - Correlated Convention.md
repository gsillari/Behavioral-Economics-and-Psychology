# Skyrms 2014 - Chapter 04 - Correlated Convention

## Place in the work

Chapter 4 is the direct continuation of the Schelling-Lewis branch inside Skyrms's book. It asks how correlated conventions can emerge when symmetric rational choice leaves agents without a principled reason to choose one equilibrium over another.

## Summary

The chapter begins with the "curse of symmetry": when options are symmetric, rational choice may have no basis for selecting one rather than the other. The problem appears in individual choice, mixed equilibria, chicken, and hawk-dove games.

In classical game theory, chicken and hawk-dove have two asymmetric pure equilibria plus a mixed equilibrium. Symmetry-respecting equilibrium selection often points to the mixed equilibrium, but that outcome can be inefficient. Skyrms then introduces Aumann's correlated equilibrium. If players can condition on a shared external cue, they can coordinate on role-dependent behavior: one swerves, the other does not. Such correlated conventions can be better than the uncorrelated mixed equilibrium.

The core evolutionary question is how these correlated conventions arise. Skyrms argues that mutation, learning, noise, finite population effects, local subpopulations, and cultural transmission can break symmetry. Once a correlated equilibrium appears, it can become a stable custom or convention.

The chapter's main concrete case is ownership. Behavioral economics treats ownership-sensitive valuation as puzzling in the endowment-effect literature. Evolutionary biology observes ownership-like behavior in territorial contests. Skyrms reads ownership roles as symmetry-breaking cues in hawk-dove games. The "bourgeois" strategy - fight as owner, yield as intruder - is a correlated equilibrium. The less intuitive "paradoxical" strategy - yield as owner, fight as intruder - can also be stable, but small asymmetries in resource value or defensive advantage enlarge the basin of attraction for bourgeois ownership conventions.

## Key claims

Symmetry can make rational choice underselect among optimal or equilibrium options.

Correlated equilibria allow external cues, roles, labels, or signals to coordinate action without requiring centralized enforcement.

Evolutionary and learning dynamics can break symmetry spontaneously and turn correlated equilibria into conventions.

Ownership can be modeled as a correlated convention in which possession serves as a role cue for conflict resolution.

## Evidence or method

The chapter uses chicken, hawk-dove, Aumann correlated equilibrium, learning dynamics, reinforcement learning, finite-population reasoning, and examples from territorial behavior in animals. It also uses the behavioral endowment-effect literature as a human ownership puzzle that becomes clearer when placed in the broader evolutionary convention story.

## Why it matters for PPE

This chapter is foundational for game-theoretic accounts of institutions because it shows how conventions can emerge without explicit agreement. It also gives PPE students a way to connect property, traffic rules, ownership, salience, cultural learning, and behavioral valuation under one strategic framework.
