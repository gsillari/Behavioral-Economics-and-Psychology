# Sugden 2018 - Chapter 04 - The Inner Rational Agent

## Summary

This chapter is the sharpest single critique in the book. Sugden argues against the idea that unstable, context-sensitive, or behaviorally inconsistent choices reveal a deeper inner rational agent whose preferences policy makers may privilege over the chooser's actual behavior.

Its importance in the current vault is very high because this is the cleanest source for resisting the move from behavioral anomaly to purified-preference paternalism.

## Key claims

The first claim is that the inner rational agent is a theoretical construction rather than a discovered psychological fact.

The second claim is that behavioral welfare economics often relies on that construction when it claims to know what people really want despite their observed choices.

The third claim is that this move is normatively consequential. It does not merely interpret behavior; it authorizes policy by redescribing disagreement or instability as evidence about a better self.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it clarifies one of the deepest disputes in behavioral public policy. The issue is not only whether preferences are unstable, but whether instability entitles experts to replace actual preferences with theorized better ones.
