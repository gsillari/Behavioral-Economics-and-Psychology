# Tversky et al. 2006 - Contingent Weighting in Judgment and Choice (in Lichtenstein and Slovic 2006, ch. 6)

## Summary

This chapter is a major mechanism chapter for the construction-of-preference branch. It argues that the weights assigned to attributes are contingent on the judgment or choice task itself, rather than fixed in advance.

Its importance in the current vault is that it explains why preference reversals are not just empirical curiosities. They can arise because different procedures make different attributes normatively or psychologically salient.

## Key claims

The first claim is that attribute weights are contingent on task demands rather than fully stable across contexts.

The second claim is that this contingent weighting helps explain systematic divergences between pricing, choosing, and other elicitation modes.

The third claim is that task-sensitive weighting gives a process account of [[Constructed preferences]] that is more informative than simply saying preferences are unstable.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter matters because it introduces a mechanism-level way of thinking about preference construction. The issue is not only that different methods produce different answers, but why that happens.

## Open questions

How much contingent weighting should a rational-choice theory tolerate? And when does task-sensitive weighting count as adaptation rather than distortion?
