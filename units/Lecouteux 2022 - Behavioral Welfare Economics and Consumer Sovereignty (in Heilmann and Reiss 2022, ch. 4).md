# Lecouteux 2022 - Behavioral Welfare Economics and Consumer Sovereignty (in Heilmann and Reiss 2022, ch. 4)

## Summary

This chapter is one of the cleanest philosophy-facing summaries in the current vault of the welfare debate inside behavioral economics. Its central question is how far behavioral findings undermine the classical ideal of [[Consumer sovereignty]] and what should replace that ideal, if anything.

## Key claims

The first claim is that standard welfare economics relies heavily on the idea that welfare is tracked by the satisfaction of consumer preferences.

The second claim is that behavioral welfare economics challenges that picture by treating inconsistent, context-sensitive, or self-defeating choice as evidence that observed behavior may fail to express welfare cleanly.

The third claim is that this challenge often relies on a controversial step: the move from unstable behavior to a reconstructed latent or truer preference ordering.

The fourth claim is that this move is neither psychologically innocent nor normatively unproblematic. That is why the chapter is especially useful next to the existing Bernheim and Sugden material in the vault.

## Why it matters for the vault

This chapter matters because it gives the welfare-and-policy branch a cleaner philosophical spine. It helps show that disputes over paternalism and behavioral public policy are also disputes over what becomes of [[Consumer sovereignty]] once revealed choice becomes less trustworthy.
