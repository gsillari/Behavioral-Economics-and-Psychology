# Shafir et al. 1997: Money illusion

## Summary

[[Shafir et al. 1997]] is the anchor source for [[Money illusion]]. It argues that people often evaluate economic situations partly in nominal rather than purely real terms, and that this can systematically distort judgments about wages, prices, contracts, and welfare.

In the current vault, the paper matters because it brings a macroeconomically important concept into the behavioral architecture without reducing it to a simple arithmetic mistake. The paper's deeper claim is representational: people often think in both nominal and real terms, but the nominal representation exerts undue force.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Shafir et al. 1997]] matters because it shows how behavioral economics reaches beyond micro choice anomalies into inflation, contracts, and macroeconomic interpretation. It matters for economics because money illusion challenges the assumption that agents naturally reason in fully real terms. It matters for philosophy because it raises questions about representation, welfare, and rational evaluation of value. And it matters for policy because wage rigidity, inflation communication, taxation, and public interpretation of economic change are all affected by how people frame nominal versus real outcomes.
