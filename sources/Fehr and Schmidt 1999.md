# Fehr and Schmidt 1999: A theory of fairness, competition, and cooperation

## Summary

[[Fehr and Schmidt 1999]] is one of the central formal sources for [[Social preferences]]. In the current vault, it matters because it gives the social branch a clear [[Inequity aversion|inequity-aversion]] model that can explain why concern with fairness changes behavior across bargaining, competition, and cooperation problems.

The paper's importance is not only that it adds fairness to utility. It also shows how the surrounding institutional environment changes whether fair-minded or self-interested behavior dominates equilibrium outcomes. In bilateral games such as the [[Ultimatum game]], inequity aversion can support fair offers and costly rejection of unfair ones. In competitive market settings, the same fairness motives may be muted because competition changes who can punish whom and what low offers mean. That gives the vault a stronger bridge from laboratory social motives to market and institutional analysis.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Fehr and Schmidt 1999]] matters because it makes one of the field's most influential departures from narrow self-interest precise. It matters for economics because it gives a tractable model of inequity aversion. It matters for philosophy because it pressures the assumption that rational agency is exhausted by self-regarding welfare. And it matters for politics and policy because institutions often depend on whether fairness concerns are activated, muted, or exploited.
