# de Finetti 1937: La prevision
## Summary

[[de Finetti 1937]] is a foundational paper on the meaning of probability under uncertainty. Bruno de Finetti argues that probability should be understood as a coherent expression of subjective expectation rather than as a purely objective feature of the world. In the current vault, the paper matters as part of the formal benchmark side of rationality: before behavioral economics criticizes human judgment under uncertainty, de Finetti helps define what rational belief under uncertainty is supposed to look like.

## Key claims

The paper's central claim is that probability is tied to personal degrees of belief, but those beliefs are not unconstrained. They must satisfy coherence conditions, famously linked to betting-rate consistency and the later Dutch-book tradition. A second claim is that prediction has both logical and subjective sources: formal rules matter, but uncertainty cannot always be reduced to objective frequencies alone. In the current vault, this matters because it makes rationality under uncertainty partly a matter of coherent belief, not only coherent preference.

## Evidence and methods

This is a conceptual and mathematical paper rather than an empirical study. de Finetti develops a philosophical and formal account of probability by analyzing prediction, coherence, and the logic of uncertain belief. In the current vault, it functions as a benchmark-construction source rather than as part of the anomaly literature.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[de Finetti 1937]] matters because it supplies a deep background assumption that behavioral economics later questions. It matters for economics because expected-value and expected-utility reasoning need some account of probability, for philosophy because it raises major questions about rational belief, coherence, and subjectivism, and for politics because institutions routinely rely on probabilistic judgments in forecasting, risk regulation, insurance, and public decision-making. It helps show that the rationality debate in behavioral economics is not only about preferences and biases, but also about what it means to hold rational beliefs under uncertainty.

## Links into the wiki

The main derived concept is [[Subjective probability]]. The paper also strengthens [[Expected utility theory]] by clarifying the belief side of the classical benchmark, enriches [[Nonstandard beliefs]] by giving the vault a more explicit formal standard against which later behavioral departures can be read, and supports both [[Behavioral Economics]] and [[What Behavioral Economics Thinks Rationality Is]] by making the pre-behavioral benchmark more complete.

## Open questions

How much of behavioral economics should be read as a challenge to de Finetti-style coherence in belief rather than only to utility-based coherence in choice? Does subjective probability provide a merely formal benchmark, or also a substantive account of good judgment? And how should de Finetti's subjectivism be connected to later behavioral work on heuristics, overconfidence, and misbelief?
