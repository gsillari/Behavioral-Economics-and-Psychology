# Gigerenzer 2008: Why heuristics work
## Summary

[[Gigerenzer 2008]] argues that heuristics work because they are adapted to environments rather than because they approximate a single universal formal standard. The paper's central distinction is between the [[Adaptive toolbox]], which descriptively studies the selection and structure of heuristics, and [[Ecological rationality]], which prescriptively studies the environments in which specific heuristics succeed or fail. In the current vault, this source is the primary ecological-rationality counterpart to the [[Heuristics and Biases]] tradition.

## Key claims

The paper claims that heuristics are not merely crude substitutes for optimization. They can be effective because cognition is equipped with a repertoire of simple rules and building blocks suited to recurring environments. It also claims that the study of heuristics should be separated into two linked projects: a descriptive account of the mind's toolbox and a prescriptive account of environmental fit. The article therefore pushes back against the idea that more information, more computation, or closer approximation to formal optimization is always better.

## Evidence and methods

This is a synthetic theoretical article rather than a single new experiment. It organizes a body of work on simple heuristics into a unified framework and uses examples from real-world decision making to show how heuristics can succeed precisely because they ignore part of the available information. The abstract emphasizes that the framework has already been used to design heuristics and environments that improve professional decision making in practice.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Gigerenzer 2008]] is essential because it prevents behavioral economics from collapsing into a simple catalog of human error. It matters for economics because it offers an alternative way to think about bounded rationality, for philosophy because it challenges the idea that one formal benchmark should govern every judgment task, and for politics and policy because it suggests that better institutional design may come from matching decision rules to environments rather than merely correcting departures from optimization.

## Links into the wiki

The most important derived pages are [[Ecological rationality]], [[Adaptive toolbox]], and [[Gerd Gigerenzer]]. The source also updates [[Rationality wars]], because it supplies a primary statement of the ecological position, and [[Heuristics and Biases]], because it clarifies the alternative benchmark against which the bias program is criticized. After [[Dekker and Remic 2019]], the vault should read this source specifically as the individual-level, heuristic-centered version of ecological rationality rather than as the whole ecological camp.

## Open questions

One open question is how far the adaptive-toolbox framework can be formalized without losing the very ecological sensitivity it is meant to preserve. Another is when simple heuristics genuinely outperform more information-intensive models and when they merely look attractive because the comparison standard is badly chosen. A third is how directly this framework should guide policy, rather than only explanation.
