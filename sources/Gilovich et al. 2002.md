# Gilovich et al. 2002: Heuristics and biases
## Summary

[[Gilovich et al. 2002]] is the top-level work note for *Heuristics and Biases: The Psychology of Intuitive Judgment*, the second major heuristics-and-biases volume after the blue book [[Kahneman et al. 1982]]. In the current vault, it matters as a foundational successor volume: not the place where the program is first announced, but the place where it is extended, reinterpreted, criticized from within, and connected more explicitly to applications, dual-process theory, and ecological challenge.

The green book should not be treated as a simple chapter container. Several of its most important chapters are already represented in the vault under their own author-year identities, and that is the right convention to keep. The book note therefore functions mainly as a structured family page: it shows which parts of the green book are already present, which chapters have now been selectively ingested from the volume itself, and which chapters still remain worth adding later.

## Structural map

The volume has three large parts:

- theoretical and empirical extensions
- new theoretical directions
- real-world applications

That organization matters because the green book is broader than a second round of famous biases. It revisits canonical mechanisms, sharpens several process distinctions, opens a more explicit dual-process branch, gives ecological rationality a direct place inside the same family, and closes with an applications section that shows how the program travels into expert judgment, finance, and public reasoning.

## Why the volume matters

The first contribution of the green book is historical succession. If the blue book consolidated the classic heuristics-and-biases program, the green book shows what happened next: mechanism clarification, theoretical branching, ecological challenge, and applied reach.

The second contribution is interpretive. The volume makes it easier to see that heuristics-and-biases did not simply remain a list of famous effects. It became a more self-conscious research program with debates about process, automaticity, debiasing, calibration, ecological fit, and the comparative reach of formal norms.

The third contribution is architectural. The green book is one of the best places in the vault to show that the heuristics-and-biases tradition and its critics belong to one evolving family rather than to two totally disconnected literatures.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this work matters because it shows the heuristics-and-biases tradition in a mature phase. It matters for economics because it strengthens the judgment-and-belief side of behavioral economics beyond the original 1970s demonstrations. It matters for philosophy because it sharpens the relation between benchmark norms, process models, and rationality critique. It matters for politics because the later parts of the volume make clear that these judgment mechanisms matter in expert settings, public risk communication, and institutional reasoning rather than only in classroom puzzles.

In the current vault, the green book should therefore be treated as one of the foundational background works for [[Heuristics and Biases]], alongside the blue book and later synthetic pages such as [[Fischhoff and Broomell 2020]].
