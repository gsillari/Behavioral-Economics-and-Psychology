# Dekker and Remic 2019: Two types of ecological rationality
## Summary

[[Dekker and Remic 2019]] argues that ecological rationality, as used in behavioral economics and related fields, does not name one coherent research program. It names two importantly different ones. The first type, associated with [[Gerd Gigerenzer]], treats ecological rationality as the success of individual cognitive strategies, especially heuristics, in real-world decision environments. The second type, associated with [[Vernon L. Smith]], treats ecological rationality as a property of systems made up of interacting individuals, institutions, rules, and norms.

In the current vault, this source matters because it prevents the ecological branch from being treated as if Smith simply extended Gigerenzer. The paper's central claim is that the two programs differ not only in level of analysis, but also in their underlying psychology, their main explanatory targets, and their experimental methods.

## Key claims

The first claim is that there are two rival meanings of ecological rationality in the literature: an individual-level heuristic ecology and a system-level institutional ecology.

The second claim is that the Gigerenzer program is rooted in Brunswikian functionalism and is mainly concerned with how bounded individuals use cues and heuristics to achieve tasks in real-world environments.

The third claim is that the Vernon Smith program is better understood through distributed cognition and is mainly concerned with how interaction, institutions, and norms generate rational outcomes at the level of social systems.

The fourth claim is that these two programs imply different experimental practices. Gigerenzer-style work favors representative design and real-world task environments, whereas Smith-style work varies institutional rules in stylized market settings to study emergent order.

The fifth claim is that the difference matters for how psychology and economics are combined. Ecological rationality is not one unified alternative to heuristics-and-biases, but at least two different alternatives sharing a common rival.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Dekker and Remic 2019]] matters because it makes the ecological side of the [[Rationality wars]] more precise. It matters for philosophy because environment-sensitive rationality can be understood either at the level of individual judgment or at the level of institutions and social order. It matters for economics because it distinguishes a psychology-of-heuristics program from a Hayekian-experimental account of markets and emergent rules. It matters for policy because these two versions of ecological rationality do not imply the same lessons about intervention, design, and institutional humility.
