# Starmer 2000: Developments in non-expected utility theory

## Summary

[[Starmer 2000]] is a major review of the literature on alternatives to [[Expected utility theory]]. In the current vault, it matters because it shows that the road from [[Allais paradox]] to [[Prospect Theory]] was not a simple jump from one classical model to one behavioral replacement. It was a broader hunt for descriptive theories of risky choice that included generalized-utility approaches, disappointment and regret models, and theories with decision weights.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Starmer 2000]] matters because it organizes a technically messy branch into a readable map. It matters for economics because it surveys the main attempts to repair or replace expected utility. It matters for philosophy because it shows how descriptive challenge, normative benchmark, and model selection come apart. And it matters for the current vault because it prevents the risk branch from collapsing into a simple binary of expected utility versus prospect theory.
