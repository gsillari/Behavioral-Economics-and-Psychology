# Schwarz and Vaughn 2002: The availability heuristic revisited
## Summary

[[Schwarz and Vaughn 2002]] is one of the most useful later sources for [[Availability heuristic]] because it reorganizes the branch around a distinction the original literature left somewhat blurred: the content that comes to mind and the subjective ease with which it comes to mind are not the same signal.

In the current vault, this chapter matters because it adds a whole class of experiments to the availability branch. Availability is no longer only about which examples can be recalled. It is also about whether the experience of easy or difficult recall is itself taken as information. That gives the concept a cleaner process structure and helps explain why some recall manipulations backfire.

## Key claims

The first claim is that recalled content and experienced ease of recall can point in different directions. People may retrieve many examples but experience that retrieval as difficult, or retrieve fewer examples but experience the task as easy.

The second claim is that people sometimes use the experience of ease as a cue in its own right. In those cases, judgment depends less on the content recalled and more on the metacognitive feeling attached to recall.

The third claim is that the relative weight of content and ease depends on processing conditions. That matters in the current vault because it makes [[Availability heuristic]] more process-sensitive and helps connect it to later questions about motivation, reflection, and correction.

## Why it matters for availability

This chapter deepens the older branch anchored by [[Tversky and Kahneman 1973]]. The classic formulation already connected availability to recall, imaginability, and associative structure. [[Schwarz and Vaughn 2002]] matters because it distinguishes two different channels inside that general family: what comes to mind and how the act of recalling feels.

That distinction is especially important for the vault because it helps explain why availability sometimes looks like a memory-content effect and sometimes like a metacognitive experience effect. It also gives the branch a more explicit bridge to domains like self-assessment, public judgment, and social inference, where ease itself can be misread as evidence.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this source matters because it shows that later behavioral work often becomes more precise by decomposing one famous heuristic into multiple process channels. It matters for economics because judgments influenced by fluency or ease may diverge from judgments influenced by actual sample content. It matters for philosophy because it raises questions about when phenomenology should count as evidence. It matters for politics because easily recalled cases and smoothly processed examples can shape public judgment in ways not reducible to their actual frequency.
