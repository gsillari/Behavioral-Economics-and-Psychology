# Hedden 2021: On statistical criteria of algorithmic fairness
## Summary

[[Hedden 2021]] is a philosophical analysis of statistical fairness criteria for predictive algorithms. It responds to the fairness-impossibility literature by asking what lesson we should actually draw from the incompatibility of different criteria. In the current vault, the paper matters because it deepens the fairness branch beyond the simple thought that "all the metrics conflict."

Its central claim is more revisionist: many widely discussed statistical criteria are not necessary conditions for fairness at all, whereas calibration has a privileged status. That makes the paper an important counterweight inside the current algorithmic-bias branch.

## Key claims

The first claim is that many purported statistical fairness criteria are not genuinely required for fairness.

The second claim is that impossibility results should not simply be read as tragic proof that all fairness ideals are mutually frustrating in equal measure.

The third claim is that calibration has a special claim to significance among the statistical criteria.

The fourth claim is that some criteria can be violated even by a manifestly fair and uniquely optimal predictive algorithm.

The fifth claim is that fairness analysis needs to separate what is merely statistically attractive from what is morally necessary.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Hedden 2021]] matters because it is a good example of philosophical sorting inside a technical debate. It matters for philosophy because it evaluates statistical criteria as candidates for moral necessity. It matters for public policy because it warns against treating every parity condition as equally authoritative.
