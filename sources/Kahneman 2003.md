# Kahneman 2003: Maps of bounded rationality
## Summary

[[Kahneman 2003]] is a foundational field-defining synthesis. In the current vault, it matters because it is not just another empirical paper by [[Daniel Kahneman]]. It is one of the clearest statements of how behavioral economics should understand its own psychological foundations. The lecture brings together intuitive judgment, choice under risk, reference dependence, framing, and bounded rationality into one overarching map.

+The source is especially important because it links the early anomaly program to a broader vision of behavioral economics. The 1974 heuristics paper and the 1979 prospect-theory paper remain the canonical primary sources for their specific domains, but this lecture shows how Kahneman himself connects them. That makes it one of the best course-level sources in the vault for explaining what the field is trying to do at all.

## Key claims

The first claim is that rationality in economics is bounded not only by information and computation in the abstract, but by stable features of human intuition and attention. Judgment and choice are shaped by psychological processes that do not mirror formal norms directly.

The second claim is that intuitive thought operates through mechanisms such as accessibility and [[Attribute substitution]]. People often answer a hard question by unconsciously substituting an easier one, then treat the resulting answer as if it spoke directly to the original problem. In the current vault, this matters because it deepens the mechanism story behind the older [[Heuristics and Biases]] branch.

The third claim is that the same psychological orientation also helps explain risky choice. [[Prospect Theory]], [[Reference dependence]], [[Loss aversion]], and [[Framing effects]] are not isolated effects. They are part of a broader picture in which evaluation depends on representation, salience, and the coding of outcomes as gains or losses.

The fourth claim is that behavioral economics should be understood as psychology for economics rather than as a mere list of deviations from the standard model. That is why this source deserves foundational status in the current vault.

## Evidence and methods

This is a synthetic lecture essay rather than a single new experiment. Kahneman revisits a large body of earlier work and reorganizes it into a conceptual architecture for behavioral economics. The paper therefore functions as a map of the field rather than as a narrow contribution to one mechanism debate.

That is exactly what makes it so valuable here. The vault already has many classic papers on particular mechanisms and anomalies. [[Kahneman 2003]] helps those papers cohere. It shows how judgment under uncertainty, bounded rationality, and descriptive choice theory fit together as one intellectual project.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Kahneman 2003]] matters because it is one of the best sources in the vault for moving between economics, philosophy, and politics without changing topic. It matters for economics because it reframes the standard model as a useful benchmark rather than a complete psychology. It matters for philosophy because it raises deep questions about intuition, normativity, and what should count as rational judgment. It matters for politics because the lecture makes clear why framing, salience, and intuitive interpretation affect institutional design and policy response.

This source is especially useful pedagogically because it is neither too narrow nor too diffuse. It lets first-year PPE students see the whole terrain of behavioral economics without losing the specific mechanisms that give the field its bite.
