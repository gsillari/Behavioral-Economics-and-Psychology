# Gigerenzer et al. 2002: How good are fast and frugal heuristics?
## Summary

[[Gigerenzer et al. 2002]] is one of the clearest ecological-rationality interventions now linked directly to the green book. Its central contribution is to defend fast-and-frugal heuristics not as second-best shortcuts to an optimizing benchmark, but as rules that can perform well because they fit the informational structure of environments.

In the current vault, this source matters because it shows the ecological challenge from inside the same family that also contains the classic heuristics-and-biases extensions. That makes it especially valuable for the border between [[Heuristics and Biases]], [[Ecological rationality]], and the broader [[Rationality wars]].

## Key claims

The first claim is that simple heuristics should be evaluated by performance in environments, not only by departure from one formal benchmark.

The second claim is that frugality and accuracy are not always in tension. In the right environments, using less information and simpler stopping rules can be an advantage rather than a defect.

The third claim is that the ecological program gains force when it offers concrete heuristic models rather than only a methodological complaint. In the current vault, this matters because it strengthens the positive side of the fast-and-frugal branch.

## Why it matters for the ecological branch

This chapter helps the vault connect the green book to the ecological side without making the latter look like a detached counter-tradition. [[Gigerenzer and Goldstein 1996]] remains the flagship concrete source for [[Take-the-Best heuristic]]. [[Todd and Gigerenzer 2007]] and [[Gigerenzer 2008]] remain the clearest later synthetic statements. [[Gigerenzer et al. 2002]] matters because it places that defense directly inside a heuristics-and-biases successor volume.

That is historically and pedagogically useful. It shows that the dispute is not merely external criticism from outside the judgment literature. It is also an internal contest over how heuristics should be modeled and evaluated.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this source matters because it makes the rationality dispute more concrete. It matters for economics because it offers a different way of judging model adequacy and decision performance. It matters for philosophy because it challenges benchmark monopoly without simply denying normativity. It matters for politics because fast-and-frugal institutional rules can sometimes outperform more complex procedures when time, attention, and information are scarce.
