# Machina 1982: "Expected utility" analysis without the independence axiom
## Summary

[[Machina 1982]] is an important attempt to preserve much of expected-utility analysis while dropping the independence axiom. Its central claim is that many of the basic tools and insights of expected-utility theory can survive if preferences over lotteries are assumed to be smooth, even when they are not linear in probabilities. In the current vault, this matters because it represents one clean response to the [[Allais paradox]]: do not abandon utility analysis altogether, but weaken the axiom that the paradox targets most directly.

The paper is especially useful because it is not framed as a rejection of utility theory in favor of a wholly different psychological currency. Instead, it asks how much of the expected-utility apparatus can be retained once independence is relaxed. That makes it a contained but important neighboring source to [[Expected utility theory]] and [[Prospect Theory]].

## Key claims

The first claim is that expected-utility analysis depends less tightly on the independence axiom than standard presentations often suggest.

The second claim is that a smooth preference representation over probability distributions can recover many familiar analytical tools even without linearity in probabilities.

The third claim is that this more general framework can help organize a range of anomalies, including the [[Allais paradox]], without requiring a full move to reference dependence or regret-based evaluation.

## Why this source matters in the vault

This source matters because it gives the vault a contained "repair" strategy for expected utility rather than only a replacement strategy. [[Kahneman and Tversky 1979]] moves by changing the descriptive architecture of risky choice. [[Machina 1982]] moves by retaining utility analysis and relaxing the axiom most directly under pressure from Allais-type evidence.

That makes the source especially useful for PPE students who need to see that the response to anomaly is not always revolutionary. Sometimes theory adapts by weakening assumptions while preserving analytical structure.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this paper matters because it clarifies one major theoretical option in the face of behavioral anomalies: preserve the utility framework but relax its strongest behavioral restriction. It matters for economics because it shows how much classical analysis can survive once independence is abandoned. It matters for philosophy because it raises questions about which axioms are essential to rational choice and which are dispensable. It matters less for politics and policy directly, which is why the source should stay contained in the benchmark branch rather than spread broadly across the wiki.
