# Fischhoff 2002: Heuristics and biases in application
## Summary

[[Fischhoff 2002]] is the clearest application-facing chapter from the green book currently in the vault. Its main contribution is to show that heuristics-and-biases should not be treated as a closed laboratory literature. The program matters because judgment errors, calibration problems, and framing effects travel into real domains where institutions have to communicate, elicit, forecast, and decide.

In the current vault, this source matters because it gives [[Heuristics and Biases]] a stronger application branch inside the same foundational family that contains the classic mechanisms. It therefore complements the older blue-book unit [[Fischhoff 1982 - Debiasing (in Kahneman et al. 1982, ch. 31)]] by showing what a later, more mature application-facing perspective looks like.

## Key claims

The first claim is that the value of the heuristics-and-biases program lies partly in what it lets institutions do better: communicate uncertainty, structure judgment tasks, and anticipate recurring errors.

The second claim is that applied use requires more than merely naming a bias. It requires task analysis, attention to domain structure, and careful design of judgment and communication procedures.

The third claim is that application does not eliminate theory. In the current vault, this matters because the chapter shows that the practical reach of the program depends on understanding what kind of process or distortion is actually at work.

## Why it matters for the heuristics branch

This source gives the vault a cleaner way to speak about the move from classic judgment experiments to practical domains. [[Tversky and Kahneman 1974]] and [[Kahneman et al. 1982]] established the core judgment patterns. [[Fischhoff 2002]] matters because it asks what those patterns become once the question is no longer whether a bias exists, but how to work with it in applied settings.

That makes the chapter especially useful for relating [[Heuristics and Biases]] to later pages such as [[Behavioral public policy]] and to the broader judgment-and-decision framework represented by [[Fischhoff and Broomell 2020]].

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this source matters because it shows how a research program becomes institutionally relevant without collapsing into pop psychology. It matters for economics because many policy and market settings depend on expert elicitation, public understanding, and forecast quality. It matters for philosophy because applied use raises questions about legitimacy, communication, and the translation of descriptive findings into intervention. It matters for politics because the practical problem is often not only whether people are biased, but how institutions should respond.
