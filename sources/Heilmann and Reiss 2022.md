# Heilmann and Reiss 2022: The Routledge handbook of the philosophy of economics

## Summary

[[Heilmann and Reiss 2022]] is the top-level work note for *The Routledge Handbook of the Philosophy of Economics*. In the current vault, the volume matters less as a source of original foundational papers than as a high-quality map of the concepts the rest of the wiki keeps using. It is therefore especially valuable for consolidation: it sharpens the history of utility theory, clarifies the formal and philosophical structure of risk, reconstructs the welfare debate around behavioral policy, and explains what economists mean by preference.

For this vault, that function is unusually important. Many central pages already exist, but some of them were built upward from classic papers and later debates rather than downward from philosophical overview chapters. This handbook helps tighten the middle layer by making the conceptual scaffolding more explicit.

## Structural map

The volume is broad, but the current first-wave ingest focuses on four chapters that directly reinforce the vault's foundations:

- Chapter 2 on the history of utility theory
- Chapter 3 on the economics and philosophy of risk
- Chapter 4 on behavioral welfare economics and consumer sovereignty
- Chapter 5 on the economic concept of preference

That is the right first pass because it strengthens the choice-theory benchmark pages, the risk branch, the welfare-and-policy branch, and the preference branch without scattering the handbook across too many domains at once.

## Main claims

At the work level, the first claim is that many disputes in behavioral economics are downstream of older disputes in utility theory, decision theory, and welfare economics. The field does not begin from nowhere.

The second claim is that formal benchmark concepts such as utility, risk, and preference are philosophically richer than they often look in classroom summaries. That matters because later behavioral criticism often assumes simplified versions of those concepts.

The third claim is that behavioral public policy and behavioral welfare economics cannot be understood well without reconstructing the older idea of [[Consumer sovereignty]] and the methodological ambitions of [[Revealed preference theory]].

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this work matters because it helps connect formal economics, descriptive psychology, and normative policy argument without collapsing them into one story. It matters for economics because it clarifies where utility theory, risk theory, and preference theory came from. It matters for philosophy because it shows why apparently technical ideas carry methodological and normative commitments. And it matters for politics and public policy because it gives the current welfare debate a cleaner background than the vault had before this pass.
