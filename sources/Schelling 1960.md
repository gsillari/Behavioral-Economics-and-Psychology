# Schelling 1960: The Strategy of Conflict

## Summary

[[Schelling 1960]] is the canonical book-level source for Schelling's reorientation of strategy away from pure conflict and toward interdependent decision. The working PDF is a later Harvard University Press paperback with a new preface by the author; the vault uses the original publication year as the canonical identity.

In this ingest pass, only [[Schelling 1960 - Chapter 04 - Toward a Theory of Interdependent Decision]] has been processed deeply. The chapter is the fundamental source for [[Coordination games]] in the current wiki and the immediate predecessor to [[Lewis 1969]] on focal points, convention, and the coordination-theoretic analysis of social order.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Schelling 1960]] matters because it shows that strategic rationality is not exhausted by zero-sum conflict, payoff maximization, or explicit agreement. Many political, legal, military, and social situations involve agents who must coordinate expectations, identify mutually salient signals, and sometimes cooperate tacitly even while their interests partly conflict.

This is especially important for the current wiki's game-theory branch. The recent fairness, trust, ultimatum, and cooperation ingests show how experimental games make social motives visible. Schelling adds a different but complementary point: even before enriching payoffs with fairness or reciprocity, strategic interaction can depend on salience, labels, convention, communication constraints, and mutual perception. [[Lewis 1969]] then makes that bridge explicit by analyzing conventions as self-sustaining solutions to recurrent coordination problems.
