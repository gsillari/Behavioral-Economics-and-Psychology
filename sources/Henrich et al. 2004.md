# Henrich et al. 2004: Foundations of Human Sociality

## Summary

[[Henrich et al. 2004]] is the book-level source for *Foundations of Human Sociality: Economic Experiments and Ethnographic Evidence from Fifteen Small-Scale Societies*. In the current vault, it matters because it turns the fairness, ultimatum-game, trust, and public-goods branch outward from laboratory regularities in student populations to cross-cultural variation in small-scale societies.

The book combines experimental games with ethnographic evidence. Its central contribution is not simply that people in many societies deviate from narrow self-interest. That point was already visible in laboratory behavioral game theory. The stronger point is that deviations are heterogeneous and patterned: game behavior varies across groups, and that variation appears connected to local social structure, market integration, payoffs to cooperation, sharing norms, and everyday forms of social interaction.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Henrich et al. 2004]] matters because it prevents a lazy universalism about social preferences. It matters for economics because it challenges the idea that either homo economicus or a single replacement such as homo reciprocans can serve as a universal behavioral model. It matters for philosophy because it raises questions about whether fairness, reciprocity, and norm compliance are universal preferences, culturally learned dispositions, or situation-specific rules. It matters for politics because institutions depend on the local social meanings of sharing, punishment, market exchange, anonymity, and cooperation.
