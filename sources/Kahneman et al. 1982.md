# Kahneman et al. 1982: Judgment under uncertainty
## Summary

[[Kahneman et al. 1982]] is the top-level work note for *Judgment under uncertainty: Heuristics and biases*, the edited volume that consolidated the heuristics-and-biases program into a durable field-defining reference. In the current vault, it matters less as a single unified argument than as a foundational source family that gathers canonical statements, adjacent social-judgment work, corrective procedures, calibration research, and early risk-perception material into one place.

For this wiki, the book should function as a structured source family rather than as a flat summary. Several of its most important chapters are already represented by standalone source notes from their original publication contexts, and additional chapter-level units are worth ingesting only where they deepen the conceptual spine of the vault. The value of the work note is therefore partly architectural: it shows how the classic heuristics literature hangs together as a volume and where the most reusable chapters sit within that structure.

## Structural map

The volume is organized into ten parts. It begins with the programmatic overview of heuristics and biases, then moves through representativeness, causality and attribution, availability, covariation and control, overconfidence, multistage evaluation, corrective procedures, risk perception, and a conceptual postscript.

## Main contributions

The first contribution of the volume is consolidation. It brought together early statements of the key heuristics, adjacent demonstrations from social cognition, and several chapters on overconfidence, correction, and perceived risk that help show the breadth of the program.

The second contribution is conceptual articulation. The volume does not only claim that people make errors. It repeatedly compares intuitive judgment with formal norms, especially probability theory, statistical inference, and calibration standards.

The third contribution is extension. By including chapters on attribution, egocentric availability, corrective procedures, and risk perception, the volume helps show how the heuristics-and-biases program moved beyond a few laboratory demonstrations into broader questions about social reasoning, expertise, and public judgment.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this volume matters because it is one of the clearest places to see behavioral economics before it becomes only economics. It matters for economics because many later behavioral claims about belief, expectation, and judgment trace back to the mechanisms organized here. It matters for philosophy because the volume is saturated with questions about rationality, norm violation, evidence, calibration, and the status of formal benchmarks. It matters for politics because the downstream topics of risk perception, debiasing, and public reasoning all become more intelligible once this volume is in view as a structured whole.

In the current vault, the blue book should be treated as part of the backbone of the judgment-and-inference branch, alongside later syntheses and field reviews.
