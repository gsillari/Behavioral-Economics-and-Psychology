# Simon 1956: Rational choice and the structure of the environment
## Summary

[[Simon 1956]] is a bridge source between Simon's original bounded-rationality critique and later ecological approaches. Its central claim is that rational choice cannot be understood only by specifying the capacities of the organism. It must also be understood in relation to the structure of the environment in which the organism searches, perceives, and acts. In the current vault, this matters because it links [[Bounded rationality]] to themes that later become central to [[Ecological rationality]].

The paper is especially useful because it begins from Simon's familiar dissatisfaction with maximizing models, but then moves in a different direction from the 1955 paper. Instead of focusing mainly on satisficing, aspiration levels, and reduced computation, it asks how environmental structure simplifies adaptive choice. That makes it a real bridge text: still clearly Simonian, but now pointing toward the ecological side of the rationality debate.

## Key claims

The first claim is that organisms do not need to optimize globally in order to survive and adapt successfully. They often need only simple procedures that work given the structure of the environment.

The second claim is that the environment is not just a passive backdrop. Its richness, regularity, visibility, and branching structure partly determine what kinds of simple decision rules are viable.

The third claim is that bounded rationality should therefore be analyzed relationally: the fit between limited organism and structured environment matters more than abstract optimization.

## Why this source matters in the vault

This source matters because it deepens the Simon branch without forcing the vault to treat bounded rationality and ecological rationality as unrelated paradigms. [[Simon 1955]] is the foundational benchmark shift away from global maximization. [[Simon 1956]] adds a further step by showing that realistic rationality depends not only on internal limits but also on external structure.

That is exactly why the source should connect to both [[Bounded rationality]] and [[Ecological rationality]]. It does not make Simon a full member of the later Gigerenzer program, but it does show that attention to environmental structure is already present in Simon's work in an important way.
Read together with [[Simon 1963]], the paper also sits inside a broader methodological critique: Simon wanted economic theory to stay answerable to realistic assumptions about agents and environments, not to be insulated by Friedman's `as-if` style of defense.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this paper matters because it makes rationality more relational and less abstract. It matters for economics because it suggests that successful decision behavior depends on the match between limited agents and structured environments. It matters for philosophy because it weakens the idea that rationality can be judged independently of context. It matters less as a standalone policy source and more as a conceptual bridge in the foundations of the field.
