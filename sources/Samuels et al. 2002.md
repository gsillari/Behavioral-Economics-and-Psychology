# Samuels et al. 2002: Ending the rationality wars
## Summary

[[Samuels et al. 2002]] argues that much of the apparent conflict in the rationality wars is illusory. Focusing on the dispute between the [[Heuristics and Biases]] tradition and [[Evolutionary psychology]], the chapter claims that the rhetoric of the debate has obscured an underlying convergence on many substantive points. In the current vault, this source sharpens [[Rationality wars]] by showing that disagreements about human rationality often persist because researchers are evaluating different objects by different standards.

## Key claims

The chapter claims that the opposition between heuristics-and-biases researchers and evolutionary psychologists has been exaggerated. Once rhetorical excess is set aside and the core claims of each side are isolated, much of the apparent disagreement disappears. The authors argue that assessments of human rationality often shift because researchers are using different standards of epistemic evaluation, including accuracy-based and constrained-optimality assessments, rather than genuinely disagreeing about the same proposition.

## Evidence and methods

This is a philosophical and conceptual chapter rather than an experimental paper. It reconstructs the two sides of the debate, distinguishes core claims from rhetorical flourishes, and then shows how many of the clashes dissolve when the evaluation framework is made explicit. The method is analytic clarification: instead of adding new empirical data, it reorganizes existing disputes so that real and merely apparent disagreements can be separated.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this chapter is valuable because it teaches a disciplined way to read arguments about rationality without overstating the disagreement. It matters for philosophy because it separates genuine normative conflict from verbal or methodological confusion, for economics because it cautions against drawing sweeping anti-rationality conclusions from behavioral findings too quickly, and for politics because public-facing uses of behavioral research often depend on how strongly those findings are interpreted. It helps students distinguish a deep theoretical dispute from a dispute about standards and framing.

## Open questions

One open question is whether the disagreement really disappears once standards are clarified, or whether significant normative differences remain. Another is how far the chapter's deflationary strategy applies beyond the specific conflict with evolutionary psychology. A third is whether later debates between formal and ecological rationality can be handled in the same way.
