# Strack and Mussweiler 1997: Explaining the enigmatic anchoring effect
## Summary

[[Strack and Mussweiler 1997]] is a foundational mechanism paper for the anchoring branch. It argues that many anchoring effects are not best understood only as insufficient adjustment from an initial value. Instead, anchors can work by increasing the selective accessibility of anchor-consistent knowledge.

In the current vault, this source matters because it gives [[Anchoring]] a second major process model alongside [[Anchoring]]. That makes the branch more conceptually balanced and prevents it from being represented only through the later Epley-Gilovich adjustment framework.

## Key claims

The first claim is that considering an anchor activates anchor-consistent knowledge.

The second claim is that this selective accessibility can pull later judgment toward the anchor even without a simple serial adjustment process.

The third claim is that anchoring therefore depends in part on comparative hypothesis testing, not only on under-correction from a starting value.

## Why this source matters in the vault

This source deepens the anchoring branch by giving it a strong alternative mechanism. In the current wiki, that matters because [[Epley and Gilovich 2001]] and [[Epley and Gilovich 2005]] already argue that adjustment is selective rather than universal. [[Strack and Mussweiler 1997]] helps explain what some non-adjustment anchoring effects may instead rely on.

It therefore belongs centrally in [[Anchoring]], and it should also reshape how [[Anchoring]] is framed: not as the only serious process account, but as one important account among several.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this paper matters because it shows how much turns on mechanism. It matters for economics because prices, negotiations, and valuations can be shifted by comparison standards that alter what information becomes salient. It matters for philosophy because it complicates the idea that a bias can be understood merely from its surface pattern. It matters for politics because early benchmarks and public reference points can structure judgment even when people are not consciously adjusting from them.
