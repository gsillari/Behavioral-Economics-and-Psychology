# Todd and Gigerenzer 2007: Environments that make us smart

## Summary

[[Todd and Gigerenzer 2007]] is a compact, high-level statement of the ecological-rationality program. Peter M. Todd and Gerd Gigerenzer argue that good decision making cannot be understood by looking only inside the head; it depends on the fit between simple heuristics and the informational structure of environments.

In the current vault, the paper matters because it gives the ecological branch a short synthetic source between the more concrete fast-and-frugal work of [[Gigerenzer and Goldstein 1996]] and the more polemical or programmatic statement in [[Gigerenzer 2008]].

## Key claims

The first claim is that rationality should be evaluated ecologically rather than by a single universal benchmark of logic or optimization.

The second claim is that the [[Adaptive toolbox]] contains simple, domain-sensitive heuristics that can exploit environmental structure to produce good decisions under bounded cognition.

The third claim is that understanding when heuristics work can also guide the design of environments that support better decisions rather than merely correcting people toward formal ideals.

## Evidence and scope

This source is a short theoretical overview rather than a new experimental contribution. Its value in the current vault is clarificatory: it states the ecological program in a very readable form and ties together Darwin, Brunswik, Simon, heuristics, and environment structure in one place.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this paper matters because it makes the ecological alternative easy to state without flattening it into a slogan. It matters for economics because it shifts evaluation from optimization to fit. It matters for philosophy because it reframes what counts as rational success. It matters for politics and policy because it suggests that institutions can sometimes improve decisions by shaping environments rather than only by correcting supposed errors.
