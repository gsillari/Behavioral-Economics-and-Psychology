# Chaudhuri 2011: Sustaining cooperation in laboratory public goods experiments

## Summary

[[Chaudhuri 2011]] is a selective survey of the post-[[Ledyard 1995]] public-goods literature. It organizes the field around three connected questions: conditional cooperation, costly monetary punishment of free riders, and mechanisms that sustain cooperation without monetary punishment, such as disapproval, advice, communication, and assortative matching.

The survey matters because it makes the cooperation branch more structured. Cooperation is not a single stable trait. Public-goods groups are heterogeneous: many participants are [[Conditional cooperation|conditional cooperators]], some are free riders, and some are unconditional cooperators. Contributions can decay even among conditional cooperators if expectations about others' contributions fall.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Chaudhuri 2011]] matters because it gives the mature experimental-economics map of cooperation after the early anomaly stage. It matters for economics because it shows how institutions can use conditional motives to sustain public-goods provision. It matters for philosophy because it distinguishes cooperation as a preference type from cooperation as conformity, norm compliance, or response to expected others. It matters for politics because the design problem is not only whether people are cooperative, but how institutions select, communicate, sanction, sort, and stabilize cooperative behavior.
