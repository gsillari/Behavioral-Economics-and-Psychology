# Stanovich and West 2007: Natural myside bias is independent of cognitive ability
## Summary

[[Stanovich and West 2007]] argues that natural [[Myside bias]] is largely independent of cognitive ability. The paper studies situations in which people are not explicitly instructed to detach from their own perspective and finds that even higher-ability individuals remain strongly prone to evaluating evidence and arguments from the standpoint of what they already believe or favor. In the current vault, this matters because it blocks a simple equation between intelligence and rationality.

The source is especially useful because it sharpens a distinction that matters throughout the rationality branch. Some reasoning failures may improve with more analytic power, more working memory, or more statistical competence. But myside bias appears to be more stubborn. Stanovich and West suggest that heavily available prior beliefs, identity-relevant commitments, or entrenched knowledge structures can drive the bias even when general cognitive ability is high.

## Key claims

The first claim is empirical. Natural myside bias is robust across tasks and is not strongly reduced by higher cognitive ability.

The second claim is conceptual. Rational thought has dimensions that are not captured well by intelligence or by standard measures of computational power.

The third claim is diagnostic. Belief distortion can be driven by prior commitments and perspective-bound processing even when agents are capable of sophisticated analytic reasoning.

The fourth claim is strategic for the wider debate. Any account of rationality that leans too heavily on intelligence or general analytic skill will miss a major source of biased belief formation.

## Why this source matters in the vault

This source matters because it gives the beliefs branch a sharper, more discriminating concept than generic [[Confirmation bias]]. [[Myside bias]] is especially important for a PPE-oriented vault because it sits close to ideological commitment, policy disagreement, and argument defense. The paper also deepens the [[Rationality wars]] by showing that rationality cannot be read off from sheer cognitive horsepower.

It also complements [[Kunda 1990]] and [[Epley and Gilovich 2016]] in an important way. Those papers show how goals and preferred conclusions can steer reasoning. [[Stanovich and West 2007]] adds that even high-ability thinkers are not automatically protected against this kind of slant when they are reasoning from within their own position. That makes the paper a useful hinge between [[Motivated reasoning]], [[Confirmation bias]], and broader arguments about what rationality demands.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this paper matters because public reasoning often rewards articulate defense of prior commitments rather than impartial revision. It matters for economics because better information or greater sophistication does not automatically remove belief distortion. It matters for philosophy because it presses on the distinction between intelligence, reasonableness, and rational self-governance. It matters for politics because polarized disagreement may persist even among highly educated or cognitively sophisticated participants.
