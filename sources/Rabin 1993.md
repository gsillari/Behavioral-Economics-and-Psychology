# Rabin 1993: Incorporating fairness into game theory and economics

## Summary

[[Rabin 1993]] is an early and highly influential attempt to incorporate fairness and reciprocity into economic and game-theoretic analysis. In the current vault, it matters because it gives the social-preferences branch a second major formal anchor alongside inequity-aversion models. Where [[Fehr and Schmidt 1999]] emphasizes distributional unfairness, Rabin emphasizes how people respond to perceived kindness and unkindness.

That distinction is important for the vault because it shows that [[Social preferences]] is not one single model. It includes different ways of formalizing departures from narrow self-interest, and those models have different implications for cooperation, punishment, and institutional design.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Rabin 1993]] matters because it is one of the clearest bridges between behavioral evidence and formal economics. It matters for economics because it broadens game theory beyond materially self-interested payoffs. It matters for philosophy because it treats moralized response to treatment by others as part of action rather than as noise. And it matters for policy because fairness-sensitive interaction affects bargaining, compliance, contract design, and public legitimacy.
