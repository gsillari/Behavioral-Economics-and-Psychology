# Keren and Schul 2009: Two is not always better than one
## Summary

[[Keren and Schul 2009]] is a critical evaluation of dual-process or two-system theories. In the current vault, the paper matters because it pushes back against the tendency to treat every important cognitive distinction as evidence for two separate systems. It is therefore best read as a conceptual critique inside the [[Fast and slow thinking]] branch.

The paper is useful because it raises the standard of argument. It asks whether dual-process theorists are really identifying explanatory architecture or simply redescribing familiar contrasts, dissociations, and task differences in system language.

## Key claims

The first claim is that two-system theories are often too loose in what counts as evidence. Contrasts in behavior do not automatically justify positing two systems.

The second claim is that many purported dual-process explanations rely on flexible labels rather than sharply testable architecture.

The third claim is not that all dual-process work is worthless, but that its explanatory ambitions need much tighter scrutiny than the field often gives them.

## Why this source matters in the vault

This source belongs in the dual-process critique branch alongside [[Evans 2008]], [[Evans and Stanovich 2013]], [[De Neys 2012]], and [[De Neys 2023]]. Its role is different from De Neys. De Neys refines dual-process theory from within. Keren and Schul challenge whether the broad two-system framing is often too coarse or underjustified in the first place.

That makes it especially useful for the wiki’s philosophical architecture. It gives the vault a stronger internal critique of the language of fast and slow thinking, not only an ecological critique from the Gigerenzer side.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this paper matters because it teaches caution about explanatory overreach. It matters for economics because many behavioral interpretations rely on psychological architecture claims that may be less settled than they appear. It matters for philosophy because it is a methodological critique of theory construction, evidence, and taxonomy. It matters for politics and policy because oversimplified pictures of intuition and reflection can easily migrate into public discourse and institutional design.
