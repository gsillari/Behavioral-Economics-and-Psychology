# Camerer 2003: Behavioral Game Theory

## Work summary

[[Camerer 2003]] is a foundational monograph for behavioral game theory. Its central project is to keep the formal discipline of [[Game theory]] while replacing thin assumptions about money-payoff maximization, unlimited strategic reasoning, and context-free play with experimentally disciplined models of actual strategic interaction.

For this wiki, the book is best treated as a source family. It does not merely report anomalies. It turns experimental games into measurement devices for social motives, bounded strategic reasoning, learning, coordination, signaling, reputation, and equilibrium selection. It is therefore one of the main bridges between [[Experimental games]] and the broader conceptual architecture of behavioral economics.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Camerer 2003]] matters because it makes strategic interaction behaviorally realistic without giving up formal analysis. It matters for economics because it shows how experiments can discipline models of bargaining, trust, cooperation, learning, and coordination. It matters for philosophy because it raises questions about rationality, fairness, intention, and social meaning in strategic contexts. It matters for politics because institutions often depend on whether people trust, reciprocate, punish unfairness, coordinate, or learn from others.
