# Petracca 2017: A cognition paradigm clash
## Summary

[[Petracca 2017]] argues that the meaning of [[Bounded rationality]] cannot be understood apart from the cognitive psychology that originally underwrote it. Simon's project was not only an economic critique of full optimization. It was also built on [[Cognitivism]], understood here as a symbolic information-processing view of the mind. Petracca's central question is whether later bounded-rationality research in economics has really absorbed the post-1980s cognitive turn toward [[Situated cognition]] and [[Embodied cognition]]. His answer is largely no.

In the current vault, the paper matters because it makes a background issue explicit. Many pages already treat bounded rationality as finite, environment-sensitive, or psychologically realistic. Petracca shows that these descriptions are incomplete unless we ask what kind of psychology is doing the work. The paper therefore shifts the discussion from simple benchmark critique to a deeper dispute over cognitive architecture.

## Key claims

The first claim is historical and conceptual. Simon's bounded rationality was originally intertwined with the cognitivist view of human thought as symbolic information processing.

The second claim is that bounded rationality in economics has developed along two main Simonian lines. Petracca distinguishes a wider `Simon 1` version, associated with the "scissors" of mind and environment, from a narrower `Simon 2` version in which agents are mainly treated as limited information processors. The narrower version, he argues, is what mostly percolated into economics.

The third claim is that the post-Simonian cognitive turn toward situated, distributed, and embodied cognition has not significantly reshaped bounded-rationality theory in economics. Even later programs such as heuristics-and-biases and ecological rationality move beyond Simon in some respects while still largely retaining a symbolic information-processing worldview.

The fourth claim is methodological. The Simon-versus-situated-cognition controversy of the 1990s should be read not only as a historical episode in cognitive science, but as a "road not taken" for economics and behavioral methodology.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Petracca 2017]] matters because it clarifies that disputes about rationality are not only disputes about norms or benchmarks. They are also disputes about what the mind is and how cognition relates to environment, body, and action. It matters for economics because it makes bounded rationality look less like a single stable doctrine and more like a family of projects with different psychological foundations. It matters for philosophy because it reconstructs the clash between internalist symbolic models and more situated accounts of cognition. And it matters for public policy because different views of cognition imply different views of error, correction, environment design, and institutional support.

## Open questions

If bounded rationality moves beyond symbolic information processing, what would count as a genuinely post-cognitivist economic psychology? Can ecological rationality be reformulated in a fully situated or embodied way, or does it still preserve too much of Simon's original inside-outside frame? And should the vault eventually distinguish more sharply between bounded rationality as a normative-economic idea and bounded rationality as a cognition program?
