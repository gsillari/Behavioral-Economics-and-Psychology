# Ledyard 1995: Public goods: A survey of experimental research

## Summary

[[Ledyard 1995]] is the canonical survey of early public-goods experiments. The working PDF in the vault is the revised Caltech working-paper version of the chapter published in *The Handbook of Experimental Economics*. The source matters because it gives the cooperation branch its experimental backbone before the later punishment and conditional-cooperation literature becomes dominant.

The central finding is not that people are simply cooperative. It is that public-goods behavior is structured and sensitive. One-shot and early-round contributions are often positive, repeated interaction without additional institutional support tends to decay, communication often increases contributions, and design features such as marginal per capita return, thresholds, group size, and information can change outcomes.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Ledyard 1995]] matters because public goods are one of the clearest places where individual incentives and collective welfare diverge. It matters for economics because it organizes the experimental evidence around the free-rider problem. It matters for philosophy because it complicates the question whether agents are selfish, altruistic, confused, or norm-responsive. It matters for politics because public-goods provision is a core problem for institutions, taxation, deliberation, and collective action.
