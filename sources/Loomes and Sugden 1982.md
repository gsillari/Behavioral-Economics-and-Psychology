# Loomes and Sugden 1982: Regret theory
## Summary

[[Loomes and Sugden 1982]] introduces [[Regret theory]] as an alternative theory of rational choice under uncertainty. Its central idea is that people do not evaluate an outcome only by its standalone utility. They also compare it with what would have happened had they chosen differently, and they experience regret or rejoicing accordingly. In the current vault, this matters because it represents a more radical alternative to [[Expected utility theory]] than [[Machina 1982]]: instead of relaxing one axiom while preserving the same evaluative currency, regret theory changes the unit of evaluation itself.

The paper is especially useful because it positions itself explicitly against both standard von Neumann-Morgenstern theory and the growing anomaly literature around the [[Allais paradox]] and related effects. It is therefore a good contained companion to [[Prospect Theory]], even though the vault is not going to build out a full regret-theory branch.

## Key claims

The first claim is that choice under uncertainty depends partly on comparison across possible chosen and unchosen outcomes, not only on utility over realized final states.

The second claim is that regret and rejoicing can explain violations of standard expected-utility axioms, including patterns associated with the [[Allais paradox]].

The third claim is that this comparative emotional structure offers a simpler or more intuitive alternative to some other post-expected-utility theories, including the original presentation of prospect theory.

## Why this source matters in the vault

This source matters because it shows a second style of response to Allais-type anomalies. [[Machina 1982]] tries to save utility analysis without independence. [[Loomes and Sugden 1982]] instead shifts the evaluative basis from utility over states to comparative feelings about chosen versus forgone outcomes. That contrast is exactly the contained distinction worth preserving in the vault.

It also helps keep [[Prospect Theory]] in perspective. Prospect theory is not the only alternative to expected utility; it is just the one that became most central to behavioral economics.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this paper matters because it makes clear that anomalies in risky choice can be theorized in more than one way. It matters for economics because it changes what counts as the object of evaluation in choice under uncertainty. It matters for philosophy because it raises a clean question about rationality: should unchosen counterfactual outcomes affect the rational evaluation of a choice? Its direct policy relevance is limited in the current vault, so the source should remain close to the benchmark and theory pages.
