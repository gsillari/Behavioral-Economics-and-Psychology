# Frederick et al. 2002: Time discounting and time preference

## Summary

[[Frederick et al. 2002]] is a major review of intertemporal choice. In the current vault, it matters because it separates the standard discounted-utility benchmark from the wide range of empirical regularities that pressured it, and it does so in a way that is broader and more methodologically synthetic than any single present-bias paper.

Its particular value is that it keeps the intertemporal branch from becoming too quickly identified with one formal model. The paper reviews anomalies, alternative formulations, and measurement problems while insisting that time preference must be distinguished from many other forces that shape intertemporal choice.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Frederick et al. 2002]] matters because it makes the intertemporal branch teachable without flattening it into a single slogan about self-control. It matters for economics because it surveys the empirical and theoretical instability of discounted utility. It matters for philosophy because it raises deep questions about prudence, self-command, and personal continuity. And it matters for policy because interventions aimed at saving, addiction, education, or health often presuppose a diagnosis of what kind of intertemporal failure is actually present.
