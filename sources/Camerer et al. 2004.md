# Camerer et al. 2004: Advances in behavioral economics

## Summary

[[Camerer et al. 2004]] is the top-level work note for *Advances in Behavioral Economics*. In the current vault, the volume matters as an early canonical collection that shows behavioral economics not just as a handful of anomalies, but as a structured field with work on risky choice, intertemporal choice, fairness, game theory, macroeconomic applications, labor markets, finance, and new theoretical foundations.

Unlike some edited volumes that are mainly useful for one cluster, this book is broad enough to touch many central branches of the vault at once. That breadth is also why the ingest has to stay selective. The volume should strengthen the vault's architecture without causing five different branches to expand all at once around reprinted classics that often already exist elsewhere in the literature.

## Structural map

The volume is organized into four large parts:

- introduction
- basic topics
- applications
- new foundations

This is the right first wave because it strengthens one field-level overview, one risk-theory bridge, one intertemporal-choice bridge, two major social-preference sources, and one durable macro-behavioral concept.

## Main claims

At the work level, the volume's first claim is architectural. Behavioral economics is not only a challenge to rational choice in isolated laboratory tasks. It is a field with multiple connected branches.

The second claim is substantive. The same general research program reaches from preference anomalies and risk theory into self-control, fairness, macroeconomic perception, bargaining, finance, and strategic interaction.

The third claim is historical. Many of the chapters are reprints of landmark articles, which means the volume works best in the vault not as a container that replaces original publication history, but as a map that groups key strands into one recognizable family.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this work matters because it shows the scale of behavioral economics as it looked in the early 2000s, after the anomaly phase but before the later explosion of behavioral public policy and AI-related branches. It matters for economics because it places risk, time, fairness, labor, finance, and macro topics in one conversation. It matters for philosophy because it shows how many different kinds of benchmark pressure the standard model was already facing. And it matters for politics because several chapters already point toward institutional, macroeconomic, and policy implications rather than staying at the level of individual choice.
