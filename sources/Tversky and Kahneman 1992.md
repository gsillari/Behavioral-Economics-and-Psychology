# Tversky and Kahneman 1992: Advances in prospect theory

## Summary

[[Tversky and Kahneman 1992]] presents cumulative prospect theory as a major advance over the original [[Kahneman and Tversky 1979]] formulation. The paper keeps the core behavioral ideas of gains and losses, [[Loss aversion]], and nonlinear treatment of uncertainty, but replaces the original separable weighting scheme with cumulative, rank-dependent decision weights.

In the current vault, this source matters because it shows that prospect theory was not a one-shot 1979 model. It became a more general and theoretically cleaner framework.

## Key claims

The first claim is that the original prospect-theory insights should be preserved: outcomes are evaluated as gains and losses relative to a reference point, and risk attitudes differ systematically across those domains.

The second claim is that the original 1979 weighting scheme has important limits. In particular, it does not generalize cleanly to prospects with many outcomes and can violate stochastic dominance.

The third claim is that these limits can be addressed by cumulative, rank-dependent weighting. Cumulative prospect theory applies to risky and uncertain prospects with any finite number of outcomes and allows separate weighting functions for gains and losses.

## What it changes relative to 1979

The original [[Kahneman and Tversky 1979]] paper is foundational, but it was explicitly built around risky prospects with a small number of outcomes and a separable weighting scheme. That original architecture was powerful as a descriptive breakthrough, but limited as a general theory.

[[Tversky and Kahneman 1992]] overcomes those limits in three main ways. First, it replaces separate transformation of individual probabilities with cumulative weighting over ranked outcomes. Second, it avoids the dominance problems that troubled the original formulation. Third, it extends the theory to uncertain as well as risky prospects with any number of outcomes, making the framework more general and more stable for later applications.

## Evidence and methods

The source combines theoretical reconstruction with experimental evidence. Its value in the current vault is not only empirical but architectural: it tells us how the mature version of prospect theory should be taught once the 1979 breakthrough is already in place.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, this paper matters because it prevents a common simplification. Prospect theory is not just one famous 1979 article with a value function sketch. It is also a later effort to repair formal weaknesses while preserving behavioral realism. That matters for economics because it sharpens the contrast with [[Expected utility theory]]; for philosophy because it shows theory change within behavioral decision theory rather than only criticism from outside; and for politics because later applications of prospect theory often rely, implicitly or explicitly, on the more mature cumulative framework.

## Open questions

How much of the empirical appeal of prospect theory depends on the original 1979 framing demonstrations, and how much on the later cumulative formalization? When should the vault present prospect theory as one unified tradition rather than as two distinct stages? And which later applications really rely on cumulative weighting rather than only on gain-loss coding and [[Loss aversion]]?
