# Dawes and Thaler 1988: Anomalies: Cooperation

## Summary

[[Dawes and Thaler 1988]] is an early behavioral-economics statement of the cooperation anomaly. It begins from the standard assumption that agents are rational and selfish, then uses public-goods and social-dilemma experiments to show that this assumption misses too much: people often contribute to public goods, especially in one-shot settings and when communication or group identity is available.

The paper is useful because it frames cooperation as an anomaly without immediately reducing it to one mechanism. Cooperation may reflect altruism, confusion, group identity, ethical commitment, fear, hope, or the social meaning of discussion and promises. That pluralism is exactly why the cooperation branch should be organized around a small set of conceptual distinctions rather than a list of experiments.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Dawes and Thaler 1988]] matters because it places cooperation at the intersection of economics, psychology, and political theory. It matters for economics because public goods are the classic site of the free-rider problem. It matters for philosophy because the paper raises the question whether cooperation is irrational, moral, expressive, or preference-revealing. It matters for politics because collective action problems are central to voting, charity, public broadcasting, environmental action, and institutional legitimacy.
