# Fehr and Gachter 2000: Cooperation and punishment in public goods experiments

## Summary

[[Fehr and Gachter 2000]] is the central experimental source for [[Costly punishment]] in public-goods settings. In a public-goods game without punishment, complete free riding is the standard dominant-strategy prediction and contributions tend to decline. With punishment opportunities, subjects often punish free riders even when punishment is costly and yields no direct material benefit to the punisher.

The paper's striking result is that punishment opportunities can sustain much higher contribution rates. Free riders are punished more heavily the more they fall below the contribution level of cooperators, and potential free riders can reduce punishment by raising their contributions. In partner groups, punishment can push behavior toward very high or full cooperation.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Fehr and Gachter 2000]] matters because it shows how cooperation can be stabilized by decentralized sanctioning rather than by pure altruism or state enforcement. It matters for economics because it challenges the claim that costly punishment should disappear under narrow self-interest. It matters for philosophy because punishment raises questions about norm enforcement, fairness, anger, and the meaning of being a "sucker." It matters for politics because many institutions depend on whether people are willing to bear costs to sanction free riders.
