# Evans and Stanovich 2013: Dual-process theories of higher cognition
## Summary

[[Evans and Stanovich 2013]] is a major attempt to clarify and defend dual-process theories at a point when the framework had attracted both strong uptake and mounting criticism. In the current vault, the source matters because it gives the [[Fast and slow thinking]] branch a more explicit conceptual backbone and makes the dual-process debate part of the broader [[Rationality wars]].

The paper does not simply repeat that there are two systems. It tries to specify what dual-process theorists should and should not claim, how type 1 and type 2 processing should be distinguished, and which confusions have made the debate harder than it needs to be. That makes it an important companion to [[Evans 2008]] and a useful foil for [[De Neys 2023]].

## Key claims

The first claim is that dual-process theory should be stated in terms of two types of processing rather than as a loose grab-bag of contrasts. Type 1 processing is autonomous and relatively effortless; type 2 processing is more demanding of working memory and cognitive control. In the current vault, this matters because it gives the fast-and-slow branch a cleaner conceptual vocabulary.

The second claim is that many criticisms of dual-process theory target oversimplified versions of it. Evans and Stanovich argue that once the framework is stated more carefully, it remains a productive way of organizing evidence about reasoning and judgment.

The third claim is that debates over rationality cannot be separated from debates over architecture. If we disagree about whether intuitive and reflective processes are sharply distinct, interacting, or overlapping, we also change how we interpret bias, norm sensitivity, and correction. That makes the paper valuable beyond cognitive psychology narrowly construed.

## Evidence and methods

This is a theoretical and synthetic article. Evans and Stanovich review the major points of contention in dual-process research and propose a more disciplined vocabulary for the framework. The source therefore functions less as new evidence and more as a conceptual cleanup and defense of a widely used explanatory model.

That is useful in the current vault because the dual-process branch had begun to rely mainly on later critical sources. [[Evans and Stanovich 2013]] supplies the internal self-clarification of the framework before the later De Neys-style refinements push it further.

## Why it matters for PPE

For PPE students, [[Evans and Stanovich 2013]] matters because it shows how a widely used picture of human reasoning is defended at the theoretical level, not only popularized. It matters for economics because the interpretation of bounded rationality often depends on whether errors are attributed to type 1 dominance, weak type 2 engagement, or a more complex interaction. It matters for philosophy because the framework bears directly on the relation between intuition, reflection, and norm-governed reasoning. It matters for politics because many arguments about citizen error, expertise, and debiasing assume some distinction between automatic and controlled cognition.

The source is especially useful in a PPE setting because it helps students avoid caricature. It shows how cognitive frameworks are stabilized, defended, and reformulated when they come under criticism.

## Open questions

How much of dual-process theory survives once overlap, interaction, and norm sensitivity are taken seriously? Is the type 1 versus type 2 distinction explanatory enough for current behavioral research? And how should the vault connect this framework to the ecological-rationality critique without forcing them into a false binary?
