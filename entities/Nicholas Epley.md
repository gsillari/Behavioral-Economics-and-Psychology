# Nicholas Epley

## Who or what it is

[[Nicholas Epley]] is a behavioral scientist at the University of Chicago Booth School of Business whose research centers on social cognition and human inference about other minds.
He is known for work on egocentric inference, anthropomorphism, perspective taking, and the mechanisms underlying motivated and intuitive judgment.
His work adds process detail to the question of how people actually generate and revise judgments.

## Why it matters here

Epley matters here because he helps move the anchoring branch beyond a flat claim that estimates get pulled toward arbitrary values. In the current vault, his work is important for distinguishing self-generated anchors from externally provided anchors, for clarifying when effortful adjustment and debiasing actually matter, and for showing that reflective cognition can also support preferred conclusions through motivated inference.

## Key relationships

The tightest connections are to [[Anchoring]], [[Anchoring]], [[Motivated reasoning]], and [[Heuristics and Biases]]. His work also links naturally to [[Fast and slow thinking]] because it identifies cases where reflective effort can matter, but not always in a cleanly debiasing way.
