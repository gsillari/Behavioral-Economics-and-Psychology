# Dan Sperber

## Who or what it is

[[Dan Sperber]] is a French social and cognitive scientist whose work spans anthropology, philosophy, psychology, and cognitive science. He is known for the epidemiology of representations and, with [[Hugo Mercier]], for the [[Argumentative theory of reasoning]], which treats reasoning as adapted for social justification and evaluation. His work is important because it offers one of the strongest alternatives to the view that reasoning is primarily an individual truth-tracking device.

## Why it matters here

Sperber matters here because his work helps make reasoning a social and communicative phenomenon rather than only an individual computational process. That gives the vault a different way to understand bias, justification, and epistemic conflict.

## Key relationships

The closest links are to [[Argumentative theory of reasoning]], [[Rationality wars]], and [[Behavioral Economics]]. Through the argumentative branch, his work also bears on how the vault interprets [[Motivated reasoning]], [[Confirmation bias]], and the limits of purely benchmark-based models of rationality.
