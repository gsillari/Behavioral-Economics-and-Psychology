# Milton Friedman

## Who or what it is

[[Milton Friedman]] (1912-2006) was an American economist and public intellectual associated with the Chicago School.
He is one of the twentieth century's most influential defenders of positive economics, predictive success, and the use of simplified models.
He matters to behavioral economics chiefly as a methodological benchmark and foil rather than as a contributor to its substantive findings.
In the current vault he also matters because later critics such as [[Berg and Gigerenzer 2010]] argue that behavioral economics often remained closer to Friedman's `as-if` methodology than it publicly acknowledged.

## Why this entity matters

For PPE students, Friedman matters not only for policy debates but for the philosophy of economics. He helps define the methodological background against which later disputes about bounded rationality, descriptive adequacy, behavioral realism, and the `as-if` methodology become sharper.
