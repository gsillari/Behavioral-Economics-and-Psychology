# Gerd Gigerenzer

## Who or what it is

[[Gerd Gigerenzer]] (born 1947) is a German psychologist best known for developing the ecological-rationality and fast-and-frugal-heuristics program.
He led the Center for Adaptive Behavior and Cognition at the Max Planck Institute for Human Development after earlier work at the University of Chicago, the University of Konstanz, and the Max Planck Institute for Psychological Research.
Gigerenzer argues that the quality of reasoning should be judged by fit to environments rather than by a single abstract standard of formal optimization.
That position made him one of the strongest critics of treating human judgment primarily as a catalogue of biases.
His work on the [[Adaptive toolbox]], [[Take-the-Best heuristic]], and risk literacy gave the ecological side of the rationality debates its clearest positive program.
He has also been influential outside the academy through public work on medical statistics, uncertainty, and health communication.
More recently he has been affiliated with the Harding Center for Risk Literacy at the University of Potsdam and with projects aimed at improving public understanding of risk.
Gigerenzer matters because he offered not just a critique of bias-based models but a rival picture of how bounded agents can reason well.
That makes him a central figure wherever behavioral economics meets philosophy of rationality, institutional design, and real-world decision tools.
He is one of the clearest representatives of the claim that simple heuristics can be intelligent rather than merely defective.

## Why it matters here

Gigerenzer matters here because he gives the vault a clear primary source for the main alternative to the bias-centered view of human reasoning. His work helps the vault avoid treating the [[Heuristics and Biases]] tradition as the only serious framework for understanding heuristics, rationality, and policy. The newer reflection source also helps show how this disagreement was experienced from inside the debate rather than only reconstructed after the fact. After [[Dekker and Remic 2019]], the comparison with [[Smith 2003]] should be read more sharply: Gigerenzer represents an individual-level ecological rationality of heuristics, not the whole ecological camp. [[Berg and Gigerenzer 2010]] adds a second role: Gigerenzer is not only offering an alternative benchmark, but also a methodological critique of behavioral economics for preserving a Friedman-style `as-if` architecture under behaviorally richer labels.

## Key relationships
