# John A. List

## Who or what it is

[[John A. List]] (born 1968) is an American economist known for turning field experiments into a major tool of empirical economics.
He is a professor at the University of Chicago and has done influential work in environmental economics, charitable giving, education, firms, and market behavior.
List is especially important for showing how behavioral anomalies look different in natural markets than they do in laboratory settings.
His studies of trading experience and the [[Endowment effect]] made him a central figure in the field-evidence branch of behavioral economics.
He is one of the key researchers who tests whether canonical effects survive contact with real institutions and real incentives.

## Why it matters here

List matters here because his work tests whether classic laboratory anomalies survive in actual markets and how market experience changes behavior. In the current vault, that makes him a key figure in connecting behavioral theory to field evidence and institutional learning. [[Laibson and List 2015]] also gives him a different role in the vault: not only as a field experimentalist, but as a clear expositor of the field's main teaching principles.
