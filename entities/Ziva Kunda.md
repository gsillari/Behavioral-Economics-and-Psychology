# Ziva Kunda

## Who or what it is

[[Ziva Kunda]] (1955-2004) was an Israeli social psychologist who taught at Princeton University and the University of Waterloo.
She is best known for her classic theoretical work on [[Motivated reasoning]] and for broader contributions to social cognition.
Her research helped show how goals and desired conclusions can shape the very process of belief formation.

## Why it matters here

Kunda matters here because she gives the beliefs branch a richer mechanism than simple error or ignorance. In the current wiki, her work helps explain how people can reason in a way that is structured, evidentially constrained, and still directionally biased.

## Key relationships

The tightest links are to [[Motivated reasoning]], [[Nonstandard beliefs]], and [[Heuristics and Biases]]. Her work also belongs near [[Judgment, Belief, and Inference]] because it shows that inferential distortion can come from goals as well as from shortcut-based processing.
