# Elinor Ostrom

## Who or what it is

[[Elinor Ostrom]] was a political economist and institutional theorist whose work transformed the study of commons governance. In this wiki, her immediate role is through [[Ostrom 2008]], a compact reconstruction of how the [[Tragedy of the commons]] evolved after [[Hardin 1968]].

Ostrom matters here because she turns the commons problem from a simple prediction of inevitable overuse into a comparative institutional question. Common-pool resources can be overused under open access, but users sometimes create rules, boundaries, monitoring arrangements, and sanctions that sustain cooperation. This makes her central to [[Common-pool resources]], [[Cooperation]], [[Free riding]], and [[Behavioral public policy]].

## Why it matters here

Ostrom's contribution is not merely optimistic. She does not say commons always self-govern successfully. She argues that success and failure depend on conditions: communication costs, group size, discount rates, homogeneity of interests, authority to make rules, legitimacy, monitoring, and fit between institutional boundaries and ecological boundaries.

For the vault, she is the key figure who prevents [[Tragedy of the commons]] from becoming a one-source Hardin parable. Her work opens the branch toward institutional diversity, self-governance, no-panacea policy analysis, and the empirical study of when cooperation is made credible.
