# Paul Slovic

## Who or what it is

[[Paul Slovic]] (born 1938) is an American psychologist and decision researcher who helped build modern work on risk, valuation, and preference construction.
He co-founded Decision Research, served as its president, and taught psychology at the University of Oregon.
His work on [[Risk perception]] helped establish the psychometric study of how laypeople understand hazards, dread, and uncertainty.
He also played a major role in showing that values are often constructed in the course of elicitation rather than simply revealed from stable underlying tastes.
Across psychology, risk analysis, and public policy, he is one of the key figures linking judgment research to practical questions about hazard, value, and public choice.

## Why it matters here

Slovic matters here because he gives the vault both a foundational account of [[Risk perception]] and a high-level articulation of why preference-construction findings matter for rational choice, welfare analysis, and institutional design. The blue-book chapter [[Slovic et al. 1982 - Facts versus Fears (in Kahneman et al. 1982, ch. 33)]] is especially useful because it shows an earlier, policy-facing form of that contribution: public risk judgment cannot be understood as simple ignorance of expert facts. His work helps connect experimental anomalies to broader philosophical and policy questions.
