# Vernon L. Smith

## Who or what it is

[[Vernon L. Smith]] (born 1927) is an American economist who pioneered the use of laboratory experiments in economics.
He taught at Purdue, Brown, the University of Massachusetts, Caltech, and the University of Arizona before later affiliations including George Mason and Chapman University.
Smith received the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 2002 for establishing laboratory experiments as tools for empirical economic analysis.
His work made experimental markets central to economics and later helped him argue for an institutional and evolutionary view of rationality.
He is important because he pushes the rationality question away from isolated individuals and toward rules, markets, and emergent order.
His career made experimental economics a permanent part of the discipline's empirical toolkit.

## Why it matters here

Smith matters here because he is not just a more institutional member of the Gigerenzer camp. In the current vault, [[Dekker and Remic 2019]] makes clear that Smith represents a different type of ecological rationality: one centered on markets, rules, distributed cognition, and emergent institutional order rather than on individual heuristics. That gives the ecological branch a distinctly economic and Hayekian register rather than leaving it only on the psychology side.

## Key relationships

The tightest links are to [[Ecological rationality]], [[Constructivist rationality]], [[Rationality wars]], and [[Behavioral Economics]]. His work is also an important comparison point for the Gigerenzer branch, because both use the phrase ecological rationality but for different research programs.
