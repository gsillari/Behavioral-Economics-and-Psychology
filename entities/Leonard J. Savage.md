# Leonard J. Savage

## Who or what it is

[[Leonard J. Savage]] (1917-1971) was an American statistician and decision theorist whose book [[Savage 1954]] gives the current vault its clearest foundational account of subjective expected utility under uncertainty. His central importance here is not just that he defended personal probability. It is that he integrated personal probability, utility, acts, observation, and statistical decision into one benchmark architecture.

## Why it matters here

Savage matters because many later behavioral critiques in the vault target a benchmark that is more specific than generic rational choice. [[Ellsberg 1961]], for example, is best read not as a diffuse challenge to all expected utility, but as a pressure point on the Savage benchmark for uncertainty. That is why Savage belongs at the center of the foundations branch and not only in the background of Bayesian decision theory.

## Key relationships

The tightest connection is to [[Subjective expected utility theory]], which now functions as the umbrella concept for Savage's benchmark in the vault. The other main links are to [[Subjective probability]], because Savage extends and operationalizes the personalistic tradition, and to [[Expected utility theory]], because the vault now distinguishes more clearly between the risk-side and uncertainty-side branches of the benchmark. [[Ellsberg 1961]] is the canonical early challenge source.

## Related sources

The direct source note is [[Savage 1954]]. The closest neighboring sources are [[de Finetti 1937]], which supplies the deeper personal-probability background, and [[Ellsberg 1961]], which pressures the resulting framework once ambiguity rather than measurable risk is introduced.
