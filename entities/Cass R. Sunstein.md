# Cass R. Sunstein

## Who or what it is

[[Cass R. Sunstein]] (born 1954) is an American legal scholar and public intellectual whose work connects behavioral findings to law, regulation, and democratic institutions.
He has taught most prominently at the University of Chicago and Harvard Law School, where he is the Robert Walmsley University Professor and founder of the Program on Behavioral Economics and Public Policy. Sunstein writes across constitutional law, administrative law, risk regulation, political theory, and behavioral policy.

With [[Richard H. Thaler]], he helped popularize the idea of libertarian paternalism and the practical use of defaults, framing, and choice architecture. The clearest public expression of that collaboration is now the source family around [[Thaler and Sunstein 2021]], which turns the original article-level defense of nudging into a book-length program and then revisits it after years of criticism and institutional uptake. His work made behavioral economics matter to lawyers, regulators, and policymakers rather than only to experimental economists and psychologists. He also served from 2009 to 2012 as Administrator of the White House Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs, giving him direct experience with real policy machinery.
That institutional role matters because much of his writing tests behavioral ideas against implementation, legitimacy, and administrative capacity.

Sunstein is also an unusually prolific synthesizer, turning narrow findings into larger arguments about welfare, autonomy, and public reason. In the behavioral-policy branch, he is one of the main figures who asks not only whether people err, but what institutions may do about it.
He therefore stands at the junction of behavioral economics, public law, regulation, and normative political theory.

## Why it matters here

Sunstein matters here because his work helps translate behavioral ideas into legal and institutional analysis. In the current wiki, [[Sunstein and Thaler 2003]] anchors the libertarian-paternalism branch, [[Thaler and Sunstein 2021]] now serves as its main book-length statement, and [[Nudge]] gives that branch a clearer tool-level concept distinct from the broader justificatory framework. [[Jolls and Sunstein 2006]] is important for a different reason: it shows how the policy branch can move from diagnosing bounded rationality to trying to reduce it through legal design, which is why it matters directly for [[Debiasing]] and [[Behavioral law and economics]]. [[Kuran and Sunstein 1999]] anchors the availability-cascade branch of risk regulation, [[Sunstein 2003]] deepens the terrorism-risk branch through [[Probability neglect]], [[Sunstein 2014]] gives the policy branch a book-length treatment of defaults, active choice, and the value of delegation, [[Sunstein 2025]] extends the same policy concerns into AI-powered choice engines and personalized behavioral steering, and [[Bar-Gill et al. 2023]] adds a different AI-facing concern through [[Algorithmic harm]] in consumer markets.

## Key relationships

The tightest links are to [[Behavioral public policy]], [[Availability cascades]], [[Probability neglect]], and [[Libertarian paternalism]]. [[Sunstein 2014]] is especially important because it broadens Sunstein's role from coauthor of one influential policy article to an extended theorist of defaults, choice burdens, and personalized choice architecture. [[Sunstein 2025]] now adds an AI-facing extension of that same role.
