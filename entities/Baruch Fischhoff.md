# Baruch Fischhoff

## Who or what it is

[[Baruch Fischhoff]] (born 1946) is an American psychologist and policy scholar best known for work on risk, foresight, and judgment under uncertainty. A student of [[Daniel Kahneman]] and [[Amos Tversky]] at the Hebrew University, he later became Howard Heinz University Professor at Carnegie Mellon University. His research helped define topics such as hindsight bias, calibration, risk communication, and the public understanding of expert knowledge.
He has also worked closely with public agencies on health, environmental, and security decisions, giving his work an unusually applied profile. Fischhoff is one of the main figures connecting classic judgment research to institutions, communication, and policy practice.

## Why it matters here

Fischhoff matters because he gives the vault both methodological rigor and policy range. In the blue-book branch, he is central to the calibration literature, to the study of hindsight bias, to the idea of debiasing as a research and intervention program, and to risk-perception work that links judgment to public controversy and institutional design. The newer [[Debiasing]] concept page and [[Soll et al. 2015]] both build directly on the architecture he helped establish.

## Key relationships

The tightest links are to [[Hindsight bias]], [[Overconfidence]], and [[Risk perception]], with especially strong ties to [[Paul Slovic]] and [[Sarah Lichtenstein]] in the risk and calibration branches.
