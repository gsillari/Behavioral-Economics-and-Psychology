# David K. Lewis

David K. Lewis was a philosopher whose book [[Lewis 1969]] gave a canonical philosophical account of convention as a solution to recurrent coordination problems. In the current vault, Lewis is important as the immediate successor to [[Schelling 1960]] on focal points and [[Coordination games]], and as a precursor to later game-theoretic analyses of social institutions.

For PPE students, Lewis matters because he turns a strategic insight into a theory of social order: many stable social patterns persist because they are mutually expected, self-sustaining responses to coordination problems, not because they are explicitly agreed, morally required, or legally enforced.
