# Amos Tversky

## Who or what it is

[[Amos Tversky]] (1937-1996) was an Israeli cognitive and mathematical psychologist best known for transforming the study of judgment and choice. After early work in Israel and the United States, he spent most of his mature career at Stanford University as one of the world's leading researchers on decision making. With [[Daniel Kahneman]], he helped build the empirical and conceptual foundations of the [[Heuristics and Biases]] program.

Their collaborations on representativeness, availability, framing, and conjunction errors showed that intuitive judgment departs from formal statistical and economic norms in patterned ways.
He was also a co-author of [[Prospect Theory]], which reoriented how economists think about risky choice, gains and losses, and reference points.

Tversky's work combined mathematical elegance with experimental inventiveness, which made it unusually portable across disciplines. Although he died in 1996 and therefore could not share the 2002 Nobel Prize awarded to Kahneman, he is widely treated as an equal architect of the research program that prize recognized.

He also made major independent contributions to similarity, features of judgment, and choice theory beyond his best-known collaborations. In behavioral economics, he represents the field's strongest connection between formal precision and psychological realism. A great deal of the modern vocabulary of bias, framing, and decision under uncertainty still runs through problems Tversky helped define.

## Why it matters here

Tversky matters here because the vault now opens with a deeper judgment-under-uncertainty sequence before moving to descriptive choice theory. That makes him central not only to [[Heuristics and Biases]] and [[Prospect Theory]], but also to the strengthened representativeness branch in [[Representativeness heuristic]], [[Base-rate neglect]], and [[Conjunction fallacy]], and therefore to the foundations of [[Behavioral Economics]] in this wiki. The collaboration source also adds an important qualitative point: Tversky appears not only as a brilliant coauthor, but as part of an unusually generative intellectual partnership.

Those details are worth preserving here because they help explain both the speed and the coherence of the Kahneman-Tversky research sequence.
