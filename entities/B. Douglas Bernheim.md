# B. Douglas Bernheim

## Who or what it is

[[B. Douglas Bernheim]] is an American economist whose work spans public economics, behavioral welfare economics, savings, taxation, and normative questions about policy design.
He is a professor at Stanford University and is especially associated with efforts to bring more conceptual discipline to how economists talk about welfare, paternalism, and individual error.
In the current vault, he matters less as the source of many separate leaf concepts than as a figure who helps test the philosophical and methodological confidence of behavioral public policy.

## Why it matters here

Bernheim matters here because [[Bernheim 2025]] gives the vault a clear set of guardrails for thinking about mistakes, welfare, and intervention. It is especially important for [[Behavioral public policy]], but it also bears on [[Preference]], [[Constructed preferences]], and [[Preference uncertainty]] because it resists the move from unstable choice to analyst-certified welfare correction.
