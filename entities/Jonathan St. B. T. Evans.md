# Jonathan St. B. T. Evans

## Who or what it is

[[Jonathan St. B. T. Evans]] is a British cognitive psychologist and one of the leading theorists of human reasoning.
He is best known for developing dual-process accounts of judgment and inference, especially in work on conditional reasoning, deductive thinking, and rationality.
Across several decades of research, he helped make the fast-versus-slow distinction into a serious theoretical framework rather than a loose metaphor.
His work is especially important because it links experimental findings about reasoning errors to larger questions about normativity and cognitive architecture.
Evans is one of the clearest articulators of the dual-process side of the contemporary rationality debate.

## Why it matters here

Evans matters here because he helps turn the popular fast-versus-slow picture into a more serious theoretical framework. His work gives the vault a primary source for how dual-process theory was formulated from inside the field, not only how it was later criticized or revised. His response to Mercier and Sperber also helps the vault show how dual-process theorists engaged with functional accounts of reasoning rather than simply ignoring them.

## Key relationships

The tightest links are to [[Fast and slow thinking]] and [[Rationality wars]]. His work also matters for [[Behavioral Economics]] because dual-process language became one of the main ways bias findings and bounded rationality were interpreted.
