# Daniel Ellsberg

## Who or what it is

[[Daniel Ellsberg]] (1931-2023) was an American military analyst, economist, and public intellectual best known globally for releasing the Pentagon Papers in 1971.
In decision theory, he is also the source of the [[Ambiguity aversion]] challenge associated with the Ellsberg paradox, which showed that people distinguish sharply between known risks and uncertain probabilities.
His importance to the literature lies in exposing one of the clearest limits of subjective expected utility as a model of judgment under uncertainty.

## Why it matters here

Ellsberg matters here because he gives the vault a major benchmark challenge on the belief side of rationality. Earlier formal sources help define what coherent belief and rational choice under uncertainty are supposed to look like. Ellsberg then shows that agents often resist acting as if unknown probabilities could be treated just like known ones. That makes him important not only for decision theory, but for the broader PPE question of what rationality requires in genuinely uncertain political and economic contexts.
