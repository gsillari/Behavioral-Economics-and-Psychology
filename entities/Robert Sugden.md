# Robert Sugden

## Who or what it is

[[Robert Sugden]] is a British economist at the University of East Anglia whose work spans decision theory, experimental economics, welfare economics, and political philosophy. He is best known in the current vault in two roles. First, with [[Loomes and Sugden 1982]], he is a co-founder of [[Regret theory]] and an important critic of standard expected-utility models.
Second, through [[Sugden 2018]], he becomes one of the clearest internal critics of planner-centered [[Behavioral public policy]]. That later work argues against the idea that unstable choices reveal a hidden rational self whose preferences policy makers may legitimately recover and promote. Instead, Sugden defends an opportunity-based and contractarian view of markets, institutions, and regulation.

## Why it matters here

Sugden matters here because he gives the vault two distinct but important alternatives to standard economic reasoning. In decision theory, he helps represent an alternative to standard expected utility in which regret and rejoicing enter directly into choice under uncertainty. In the policy branch, he gives the strongest current liberal-contractarian critique of behavioral welfare economics, centered on the rejection of the inner rational agent and the defense of [[Opportunity criterion]].
