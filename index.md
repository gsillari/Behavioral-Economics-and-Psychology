# Behavioral Economics Wiki

## What this wiki is

This wiki is a structured knowledge base for PPE students studying [[Behavioral Economics]]. It is built around the idea that behavioral economics is not just a list of biases. It is a field with benchmarks, rival views of rationality, empirical programs, policy applications, and philosophical implications.

The wiki is organized so you can read it in more than one way:
- by **topic**, if you want a course-like path through the field
- by **concept**, if you want to understand one idea in depth
- by **source**, if you want to trace arguments back to key papers, books, and chapters
- by **synthesis**, if you want the big debates and cross-source comparisons

## Main topic map

### 1. [[Foundations and Rationality]]

Start here for the classical benchmarks and the first challenges to them. This branch covers ideas such as [[Expected utility theory]], [[Subjective probability]], [[Bounded rationality]], and the broader [[Rationality wars]].

### 2. [[Judgment, Belief, and Inference]]

This branch covers the psychology of judgment under uncertainty: [[Representativeness heuristic]], [[Availability heuristic]], [[Anchoring]], [[Overconfidence]], [[Hindsight bias]], [[Confirmation bias]], and related inferential distortions.

### 3. [[Choice, Preference, and Decision Under Risk]]

This branch covers risky choice, valuation, and preference construction. Key pages include [[Prospect Theory]], [[Loss aversion]], [[Reference dependence]], [[Framing effects]], [[Constructed preferences]], and [[Endowment effect]].

### 4. [[Time, Attention, and Choice Architecture]]

This branch covers self-control, limited attention, and interventions into decision environments. Key pages include [[Time inconsistency]], [[Limited attention]], [[Menu effects]], [[Libertarian paternalism]], and [[Asymmetric paternalism]].

### 5. [[Social Behavior, Law, and Public Policy]]

This branch covers social preferences, experimental games, cooperation, coordination, convention, evolutionary game theory, law, welfare, and public policy. It links the psychology of choice to strategic interaction and institutional design.

### 6. [[Ecological Rationality and Fast-and-Frugal Heuristics]]

This branch covers the main alternative to bias-first interpretations of reasoning. Key pages include [[Ecological rationality]], [[Adaptive toolbox]], and [[Take-the-Best heuristic]].

### 7. [[AI, Algorithms, and New Frontiers]]

This branch covers recent extensions of behavioral economics into AI, machine learning, and new forms of judgment and decision support. Key pages include [[Behavioral economics of AI]], [[Algorithmic bias]], [[Clinical versus actuarial judgment]], [[Predictive optimization]], and [[Algorithmic harm]].
