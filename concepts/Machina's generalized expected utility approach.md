# Machina's generalized expected utility approach

## Core idea

[[Machina's generalized expected utility approach]] is the attempt to preserve much of utility analysis under risk while dropping the independence axiom. In the current vault, the anchor source is [[Machina 1982]]. The idea is that preferences over lotteries can remain smooth and analytically tractable even when they are not linear in probabilities in the expected-utility sense.

## Key distinctions

This concept differs from [[Expected utility theory]] because independence is no longer required. It differs from [[Prospect Theory]] because it does not rebuild risky choice around reference points, gain-loss coding, and decision weights. It is therefore best read as a contained repair strategy rather than a broad psychological alternative.

## Evidence and debate

[[Machina 1982]] matters because it shows how the [[Allais paradox]] can motivate a weakening of expected-utility assumptions without forcing complete abandonment of utility-based analysis. In the current vault, that makes the page useful as a benchmark-adjacent concept, not as the center of a new branch.

## Practical or policy relevance

The concept matters mainly for theoretical interpretation. It reminds the vault that responses to anomaly can be conservative as well as revolutionary. Its direct policy role in the current wiki is limited.
