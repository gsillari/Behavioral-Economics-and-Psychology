# Costly punishment

## Core idea

[[Costly punishment]] is the willingness to pay a cost to reduce the payoff of someone who violates a cooperative norm, free rides, or behaves unfairly. In the cooperation branch, it explains how [[Public goods game|public-goods]] groups can sustain contributions even when free riding is individually tempting.

[[Fehr and Gachter 2000]] is the anchor source. In their public-goods experiment, subjects punish low contributors even when punishment is costly and brings no direct material benefit. Contributions are much higher when punishment is available, because potential free riders face a credible social sanction.

## Why it matters

Costly punishment is not simple altruism. A pure altruist would not normally pay to reduce another person's payoff. It is closer to [[Reciprocity]], norm enforcement, anger at being exploited, or aversion to being the "sucker" in a social dilemma. It also connects to [[Inequity aversion]], because punishment can reduce disadvantageous inequality or restore fairness.

[[Chaudhuri 2011]] adds an important caution. Punishment can sustain cooperation, but it is not automatically efficient or normatively attractive. Its effect depends on cost-effectiveness, time horizon, antisocial punishment, counter-punishment, and whether cheaper mechanisms such as communication, disapproval, advice, or sorting can do similar work.
