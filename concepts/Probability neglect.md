# Probability neglect

## Core idea

[[Probability neglect]] is the tendency to focus on the badness or vividness of an outcome while insufficiently attending to its likelihood. In the current vault, the anchor source is [[Sunstein 2003]], which uses terrorism as the main case for showing how fear can flatten sensitivity to probability.

## Key distinctions

This concept differs from [[Availability heuristic]], where the core mechanism is ease of recall or imagination, and from [[Affect heuristic]], where judgments of risk and benefit are shaped by overall feeling. Probability neglect is the more specific claim that likelihood itself gets underweighted once attention is captured by a feared outcome. It is closely related to [[Dread risk]], but dread risk names a class of hazards whereas probability neglect names one characteristic inferential failure associated with them.

## Evidence and debate

[[Sunstein 2003]] is the main anchor because it gives the concept a clear policy setting: terrorism. The paper argues that under fear, even large probability differences may matter too little to judgment and public response. [[Gigerenzer 2004]] complements this by showing one behavioral consequence after September 11: people may change behavior in ways that increase exposure to more ordinary dangers. In the current vault, the concept should therefore be read as a bridge between individual risk judgment and institutional overreaction.

## Practical or policy relevance

This concept matters because catastrophic politics often turns on exactly this pattern. Citizens, media systems, and institutions can overinvest in vivid low-probability threats while underinvesting in higher-probability harms that lack emotional force. That makes the concept central to terrorism policy, disaster planning, and crisis communication.
