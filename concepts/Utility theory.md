# Utility theory

## Core idea

[[Utility theory]] is the broad family of economic theories that use utility to represent value, ranking, or choice. In the current vault, [[Moscati 2022 - History of Utility Theory (in Heilmann and Reiss 2022, ch. 2)]] now gives the clearest overview of how that family changes over time: from early marginalist and sometimes psychological utility, through [[Ordinal utility theory]], through [[Revealed preference theory]], and into [[Expected utility theory]] under risk.

## Key distinctions

This concept is broader than [[Ordinal utility theory]], broader than [[Revealed preference theory]], and broader than [[Expected utility theory]]. Those are not synonyms. They are later reinterpretations or descendants inside the larger history of utility.

The page also matters because it blocks a common simplification. Utility theory is not one stable object that behavioral economics later attacks. What later behavioral work challenges depends on which stage of the utility tradition is in view: introspective utility, ordinal representation, revealed choice, or expected utility under risk.

## Why it matters here

This concept matters because it gives the foundations branch historical continuity. Without it, the vault jumps too quickly from revealed preference and expected utility to behavioral criticism. With it, the sequence becomes easier to teach: utility was first a theory of value and satisfaction, later a theory of ranking, later a theory of observed choice, and later again a theory of risky evaluation. That layered history helps explain why behavioral criticism does not hit every stage in the same way.
