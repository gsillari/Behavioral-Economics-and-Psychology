# Situated cognition

## Core idea

[[Situated cognition]] is the view that cognition cannot be understood adequately as detached internal symbol manipulation alone. Thought is shaped by action, task structure, bodily activity, tools, and the environment in which agents are embedded. In the current vault, the anchor source is [[Petracca 2017]], which uses the Simon-versus-situated-cognition clash of the 1990s to show what bounded-rationality theory largely did not absorb from later cognitive science.

## Why it matters here

This concept matters because it challenges the image of agents as self-contained information processors who first build internal plans and then execute them on the world. For the bounded-rationality branch, that matters a great deal. Simon's "scissors" already made environment structure important, but Petracca argues that Simon still preserved an inside-outside picture in which cognition remains fundamentally internal and symbol-based.

Situated cognition therefore matters less as a separate anomaly literature than as a methodological challenge. It asks whether realistic models of choice should treat the environment as a passive input to internal computation or as an active part of cognition itself.

## Relation to embodied cognition

In Petracca's reconstruction, situated cognition is an early and decisive moment in the broader turn toward situated, distributed, and [[Embodied cognition]]. The SDE sequence should be read historically: situated cognition was one of the first organized challenges to classical cognitivism, and embodied cognition later widened that challenge.
