# Regret theory

## Core idea

[[Regret theory]] is the view that choice under uncertainty depends partly on comparisons between what was obtained and what would have been obtained under unchosen alternatives. In the current vault, the anchor source is [[Loomes and Sugden 1982]]. The central idea is that regret and rejoicing alter evaluation in a way that standard expected utility does not capture.

## Key distinctions

This concept differs from [[Expected utility theory]] because outcomes are not evaluated only through utility over realized states. It differs from [[Machina's generalized expected utility approach]] because it does not merely relax independence while keeping the same broad evaluative structure. It differs from [[Prospect Theory]] because the key mechanism is counterfactual comparison with forgone outcomes rather than reference-point coding of gains and losses.

## Evidence and debate

[[Loomes and Sugden 1982]] matters because it offers a clean alternative explanation of violations associated with the [[Allais paradox]] and related anomalies. In the current vault, it is best kept as a contained neighboring theory rather than expanded into a major branch.

## Practical or policy relevance

The concept matters mainly as a theoretical alternative in the benchmark branch. Its direct policy role is limited in the current wiki, though the intuition is clearly relevant to experienced choice and ex post dissatisfaction.
