# Availability cascades

## Core idea

[[Availability cascades]] are self-reinforcing social processes in which a vivid event, claim, or risk becomes increasingly salient through repeated public discussion, reputational pressure, and institutional uptake. In the current vault, the anchor source is [[Kuran and Sunstein 1999]], which extends the logic of [[Availability heuristic]] from individual cognition to collective risk regulation.

## Key distinctions

This concept differs from [[Availability heuristic]], which is primarily an individual-level mechanism of judgment. Availability cascades describe what happens when salience spreads socially and becomes politically organized. It also differs from [[Dread risk]], which names a class of feared hazards. Dread can fuel cascades, but the cascade concept is specifically about amplification through communication and institutions.

## Evidence and debate

[[Kuran and Sunstein 1999]] argues that public concern can become detached from baseline risk through cumulative social reinforcement. The main debate is whether cascades should be treated mainly as distortions of public reason or as understandable responses in environments where people must rely on social cues to assess danger.

## Practical or policy relevance

This concept matters because regulators, courts, journalists, and voters all operate in environments shaped by salience. If some risks become institutionally amplified while others remain invisible, policy may become highly responsive to vivid harms and relatively unresponsive to statistically larger but less memorable harms.
