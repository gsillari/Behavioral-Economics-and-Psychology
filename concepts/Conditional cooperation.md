# Conditional cooperation

## Core idea

[[Conditional cooperation]] is the tendency to contribute to a collective good when one expects others to contribute, and to reduce contribution when one expects others to free ride. It is one of the main mechanisms that makes [[Cooperation]] both real and fragile.

[[Chaudhuri 2011]] is the anchor source in the current wiki. The survey emphasizes that many participants in public-goods experiments are conditional cooperators whose contributions are positively related to their beliefs about average group contribution. This helps explain why cooperation can start high and then decay: if expectations about others fall, conditional cooperators lower their own contributions.

## Why it matters

Conditional cooperation sits between pure altruism and pure self-interest. Conditional cooperators are not simply selfish, because they are willing to contribute. But they are not unconditional altruists either, because their cooperation depends on what they think others will do.

This is why institutions matter. Communication, advice, social history, sorting, and punishment can all work partly by changing expectations about others. The cooperation problem is therefore not only motivational; it is also epistemic and institutional.
