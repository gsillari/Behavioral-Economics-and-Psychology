# Common-pool resources

## Core idea

[[Common-pool resources]] are resources where excluding potential users is difficult or costly and one person's use subtracts from what remains for others. In [[Ostrom 2008]], examples include fisheries, forests, water systems, firewood, grazing resources, and other natural or human-made systems that yield finite flows of benefits.

The concept matters because it separates the kind of resource from the kind of property arrangement. A common-pool resource is not automatically open access, common property, private property, or state property. Those are governance regimes that may be applied to the resource. [[Hardin 1968]] became so influential partly because it treated the commons as if common resources were normally open-access resources with no well-defined rights. Ostrom's correction is that the institutional arrangement must be analyzed separately.

## Relation to tragedy of the commons

The [[Tragedy of the commons]] is one possible failure mode of common-pool resources. It is most likely when access is open, users cannot communicate, rights and duties are undefined, monitoring is weak, and each user's private benefit from extraction exceeds her share of the collective cost.

But common-pool resources do not always generate tragedy. Users may create boundaries, rules, monitoring, sanctions, maintenance duties, and local governance arrangements. The central question is therefore not "commons or private property?" but which institutional arrangements fit the resource, the users, and the wider political setting.

## Institutional mechanisms

Ostrom emphasizes several recurrent mechanisms behind successful commons governance: legitimate boundaries, secure tenure, low communication costs, relatively homogeneous interests, low costs of reaching enforceable agreements, monitoring, and sanctioning. Users often follow costly rules when they believe others are doing so, and they may punish violations even at personal cost.

This makes common-pool resources an important bridge between [[Cooperation]], [[Free riding]], and [[Behavioral public policy]]. The behavioral issue is not only whether people are altruistic. It is whether institutions make cooperative expectations credible enough that conditional cooperators are not left exposed to free riders.
