# Cognitivism

## Core idea

[[Cognitivism]] is the classical cognitive-science view that thinking is best understood as symbolic information processing. In the current vault, the main anchor is [[Petracca 2017]], which matters because it shows that the original psychological foundation of [[Bounded rationality]] was not just a generic realism about finite minds. It was a specific architecture of mind associated with Simon and early cognitive science.

## Main features

In Petracca's summary, the core features of cognitivism are internal mental representations, symbolic language-like processing, and a model of cognition that is closely tied to computation. The broader package is often associated with centralized and sequential processing, modular structure, and a strong separation between the internal mind and the external environment.

In the Simon-Newell tradition, this picture is not an incidental detail. It is what makes problem solving, search, and bounded optimization intelligible as formal operations over represented alternatives and constraints.

## Why it matters here

This concept matters because it changes how the vault should read Simon. [[Bounded rationality]] was originally a critique of omniscient optimization, but it was also a cognitivist research program. That means later disputes over [[Situated cognition]] and [[Embodied cognition]] are not side issues. They challenge the psychological foundations of one of the vault's most important concepts.

It also matters for the wider [[Rationality wars]]. Some disagreements are not only about which benchmark to use. They are about whether cognition should be modeled as internal symbol manipulation at all.
