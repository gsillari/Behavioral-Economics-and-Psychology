# Trust game

## Core idea

The [[Trust game]] is an experimental game in which a first mover can make herself vulnerable by transferring resources to a second mover, often with the transfer multiplied, and the second mover can then reciprocate or exploit that trust. It is the main experimental bridge between [[Game theory]], [[Trust]], and [[Reciprocity]] in the current wiki.

In [[Berg et al. 1995]], the investment-game design controls for repeated-game reputation, contracts, and punishment threats. The key result is that anonymous subjects still send and return money. That makes trust and reciprocity visible as economically relevant motives rather than as decorative moral vocabulary.

## Trust is not just risk

The game also matters because it separates social risk from ordinary risk. A first mover in a trust game faces uncertainty, but the uncertainty is generated by another person. [[Bohnet and Zeckhauser 2004]] shows that people require a higher threshold for trusting another person than for taking a matched non-social risk with the same payoff distribution. The bad outcome is worse when it is a betrayal.

This is why [[Betrayal aversion]] belongs inside the trust branch. A trust decision involves not only expected payoffs and [[Risk attitudes]], but also the meaning of depending on another agent's choice.

## Place in the wiki

The [[Ultimatum game]] teaches the fairness branch through costly rejection of unfair offers. The [[Trust game]] teaches the reciprocity branch through vulnerability and return. Together, they introduce behavioral game theory without reducing it to a catalog of experiments.
