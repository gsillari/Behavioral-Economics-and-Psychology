# Embodied cognition

## Core idea

[[Embodied cognition]] is the view that cognition is inseparable from bodily activity, sensorimotor capacities, and active engagement with the world. In the current vault, the main anchor is [[Petracca 2017]], which treats embodied cognition as the later and broader development of the situated and distributed turn in post-Simonian cognitive science.

## Why it matters here

This concept matters because it sharpens the limit of the older cognitivist picture. If cognition is embodied, then rationality cannot be modeled only as internal computation over representations. Perception, action, bodily orientation, and environmental scaffolding help constitute cognition itself.

For [[Bounded rationality]], the implication is significant. Many economic uses of the concept still assume that bounded agents are basically symbolic processors with limited memory, knowledge, or computational power. Petracca's point is that this leaves the embodied turn largely outside the economics-facing bounded-rationality tradition.

## Relation to the vault

The concept is especially relevant to [[Herbert A. Simon]], [[Cognitivism]], and [[Situated cognition]], because those pages describe the background against which embodiment appears as a challenge. It also matters to [[Competing Views of Rationality]] and the wider [[Rationality wars]], where the dispute is partly about what a psychologically realistic account of reasoning should even look like.
