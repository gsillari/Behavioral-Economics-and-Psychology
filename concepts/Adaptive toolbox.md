# Adaptive toolbox

## Core idea

[[Adaptive toolbox]] is the idea that the mind contains a repertoire of heuristics, building blocks, and evolved capacities that can be recruited in different environments. In the current vault, the anchor sources are [[Gigerenzer and Goldstein 1996]], [[Todd and Gigerenzer 2007]], and [[Gigerenzer 2008]], which together treat the adaptive toolbox as the descriptive side of the ecological-rationality program.

## Key distinctions

The adaptive toolbox is about what heuristics are available and how they are structured. [[Ecological rationality]] is about when those heuristics succeed or fail in particular environments. This distinction matters because it separates the architecture of cognition from the standards used to evaluate performance. It also marks a contrast with [[Heuristics and Biases]], where the same decision rules are more often evaluated by their departures from formal benchmarks.

## Evidence and debate

The current anchor sources are [[Gigerenzer and Goldstein 1996]], [[Todd and Gigerenzer 2007]], and [[Gigerenzer 2008]]. [[Gigerenzer and Goldstein 1996]] matters because it gives the toolbox a flagship concrete heuristic in [[Take-the-Best heuristic]]. [[Todd and Gigerenzer 2007]] matters because it states especially clearly how heuristics in the toolbox exploit recurring environmental patterns rather than approximating an all-purpose optimizing rule. [[Gigerenzer 2008]] presents the adaptive toolbox as a Darwinian-inspired theory of the mind and links it to a body of work showing that simple heuristics can be effective in real environments. In the current vault, the concept matters less as a settled mechanism than as the organizing vocabulary for the ecological side of the rationality debate.

## Practical or policy relevance

This concept matters because it suggests that better decision making may come not only from correcting people toward formal norms but also from selecting or designing the right heuristic for the environment. That makes it relevant to professional decision support, institutional design, and the broader choices facing [[Behavioral public policy]].

## Open questions

How modular should the adaptive toolbox be taken to be in real cognition? Which parts of the toolbox are general-purpose, and which are tightly domain-bound? How should we compare toolbox-based explanation with more unified models of judgment?
