# Behaviorism

## Core idea

[[Behaviorism]] is the view that scientific explanation of behavior should be grounded in observable actions and regularities rather than in introspected mental states. In the current vault, the main anchor is [[Hands 2010]], which matters because it shows that behaviorism is an important missing piece in the history of consumer choice theory: the move away from hedonistic psychology did not simply produce a psychology-free economics, but in one crucial branch it encouraged a turn toward operational and observational conceptions of behavior.

## Key distinctions

This concept differs from a generic hostility to psychology. [[Hands 2010]] is useful precisely because it shows that the history is more selective than that. [[Ordinal utility theory]] rejects cardinal and introspective hedonism, but does not straightforwardly become behaviorist. The sharper behaviorist move comes in [[Revealed preference theory]], especially in Samuelson's early attempt to reconstruct consumer theory without relying on utility or inner preference talk at all.

It also differs from [[Cognitivism]]. Where cognitivism centers internal representations and information processing, behaviorism is suspicious of explanations that depend on unobservable mental states. In the current vault, that contrast helps show that economics' relation to psychology has been structured not by one simple opposition, but by choices among rival psychologies.

## Why it matters here

This concept matters because it clarifies the methodological prehistory of behavioral economics. The standard story says economics expelled psychology and later let it back in. [[Hands 2010]] complicates that story by showing that some economists did not want no psychology at all. They wanted a scientifically respectable psychology, and behaviorism seemed to offer one route.

That matters especially for [[Revealed preference theory]]. Samuelson's original program treated observable choices as the proper scientific basis for consumer theory and aimed to avoid unobservable mental states. Hands' point is not that economics became fully behaviorist. It is that behaviorism shaped one major attempt to make consumer theory more scientifically respectable after the decline of hedonistic introspection.
