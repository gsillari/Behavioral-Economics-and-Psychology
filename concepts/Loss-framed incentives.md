# Loss-framed incentives

## Core idea

[[Loss-framed incentives]] are incentive schemes in which a reward is presented as something already provisionally possessed and at risk of being lost, rather than as a gain to be earned. In the current vault, the anchor source is [[Hossain and List 2009]], which studies this logic in a Chinese factory.

## Key distinctions

The concept differs from generic bonuses or incentives because the economic amount can remain constant while the framing changes. It is closely related to [[Loss aversion]] and [[Reference dependence]] because the worker's response depends on whether the bonus is coded as a potential gain or a threatened loss.

## Evidence and debate

[[Hossain and List 2009]] shows that loss framing can increase productivity in a natural field setting, especially for teams. In the current vault, this matters because it gives the loss-aversion branch a concrete organizational application rather than leaving it in abstract choice theory. Debate concerns persistence, fairness, stress, and whether such framing should be treated as smart design or manipulation.

## Practical or policy relevance

This concept matters because many institutions design incentives for workers, students, patients, and public employees. If framing alone changes effort, then incentive design is partly a behavioral and ethical question, not only a financial one.
