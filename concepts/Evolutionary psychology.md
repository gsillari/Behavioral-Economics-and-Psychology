# Evolutionary psychology

this is a STUB

## Core idea

[[Evolutionary psychology]] is a research program that explains features of human cognition and behavior in terms of evolved psychological adaptations. In the current vault, the concept enters through [[Samuels et al. 2002]], where it appears as one side of the dispute over how rational human beings really are.

## Key distinctions

In the present cluster, evolutionary psychology is important not as a general theory of mind but as a position in the rationality wars. It contrasts with the [[Heuristics and Biases]] tradition by arguing that some findings taken to show irrationality may instead reflect the mismatch between laboratory task formats and the forms of information-processing for which human cognition evolved. That makes it related to, but not identical with, later ideas about ecological fit.

## Evidence and debate

The anchor source in the vault is [[Samuels et al. 2002]]. The chapter argues that the dispute between evolutionary psychology and heuristics-and-biases is often overstated because the two sides rely on different standards of assessment. In the current vault, this makes evolutionary psychology less a settled rival doctrine and more a lens through which to reinterpret what bias findings do and do not establish.

## Practical or policy relevance

This concept matters because judgments about human rationality affect how strongly we think institutions should intervene, correct, or redesign choice environments. If some apparently irrational behavior reflects adaptive design under ecologically appropriate representations, then the policy implications of behavioral findings become less straightforward.
