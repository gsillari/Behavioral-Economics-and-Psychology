# Inequity aversion

## Core idea

[[Inequity aversion]] is the idea that people dislike unequal payoff distributions, especially when they are worse off than others and, to a weaker degree, when they are better off. In the current wiki, it is the main outcome-based model of [[Fairness]] inside [[Social preferences]].

The anchor source is [[Fehr and Schmidt 1999]]. Their model is important because it does not merely say that people care about fairness. It shows how a simple modification of utility can explain patterns across the [[Ultimatum game]], [[Dictator game]], cooperation games, punishment, and competitive market settings. [[Fehr and Gachter 2000]] supplies the public-goods punishment evidence that makes this connection vivid.

## Why it matters

Inequity aversion gives the fairness branch one formal route back into [[Game theory]]. It explains why responders may reject low ultimatum offers: the material cost of rejection can be outweighed by the disutility of disadvantageous inequality. It also explains why some cooperative behavior and [[Costly punishment|costly punishment]] can survive when narrow self-interest predicts [[Free riding|free riding]].

But the concept should not absorb all of fairness. [[Reciprocity]] and intention-based models matter because people often care not only about unequal outcomes, but also about how those outcomes came about. That is why [[Rabin 1993]] and [[Fehr and Schmidt 1999]] are complementary rather than redundant.

## Open questions
