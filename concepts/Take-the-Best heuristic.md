# Take-the-Best heuristic

## Core idea

[[Take-the-Best heuristic]] is a one-reason decision rule that searches cues in order and stops at the first cue that discriminates between options. In the current vault, the anchor source is [[Gigerenzer and Goldstein 1996]], which presents it as a flagship example of fast-and-frugal reasoning under bounded rationality.

## Key distinctions

The concept differs from optimization models that integrate all available information and weight cues continuously. It also differs from a vague claim that people use shortcuts. [[Take-the-Best heuristic]] is algorithmically specific: search is ordered, stopping is early, and decision rests on one decisive cue. In the current vault, that specificity matters because it gives the ecological-rationality branch a concrete model rather than a general slogan.

## Evidence and debate

[[Gigerenzer and Goldstein 1996]] argues that this heuristic can perform surprisingly well in environments where cue validity is structured in the right way. The debate is not whether the heuristic always works, but when it does and why. In the current vault, this makes the concept an important companion to [[Ecological rationality]] and [[Adaptive toolbox]].

## Practical or policy relevance

This concept matters because many real decisions in medicine, hiring, triage, and public administration are made under time pressure and limited information. The page therefore helps the vault think about when simple institutional decision rules might outperform more complex but less usable procedures.
