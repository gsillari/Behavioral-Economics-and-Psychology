# Myside bias

## Core idea

[[Myside bias]] is the tendency to evaluate evidence, arguments, and possibilities from the standpoint of one's own prior belief, commitment, or favored side. In the current vault, the main anchor source is [[Stanovich and West 2007]], which argues that natural myside bias is strikingly independent of cognitive ability. That matters because it shows that a person can be smart, articulate, and analytically capable while still reasoning in a perspective-bound way.

## Key distinctions

This concept overlaps with [[Confirmation bias]] but is narrower and more positional. Confirmation bias concerns the search for and treatment of evidence that supports an existing hypothesis. Myside bias is especially concerned with defending or reasoning from one's own side of a dispute. It also overlaps with [[Motivated reasoning]], but the emphasis here is on perspective-bound evaluation rather than on the full range of directional goals. In the current vault, myside bias is best treated as a specific mechanism inside the broader family of distorted belief formation.

## Evidence and debate

[[Stanovich and West 2007]] is the key source because it shows that natural myside bias does not fall away simply as intelligence rises. This makes the page important for the [[Rationality wars]] as well as for the belief branch. It suggests that rationality cannot be identified with raw cognitive ability or analytic horsepower alone. The phenomenon also fits closely with the themes of [[Motivated reasoning]] and with the argumentative interpretation of reasoning advanced in [[Mercier and Sperber 2011]], even though that latter source is not the direct empirical anchor of this page.

The main debate is whether myside bias should be treated as just one manifestation of confirmation bias or as a distinct construct tied more closely to identity, advocacy, and perspective-taking. In a PPE-oriented vault, the second option is often more useful because it better captures political disagreement and policy argument.

## Practical or policy relevance

This concept matters because many of the most important public disagreements are not cases of simple ignorance. They are cases in which people reason capably but from within entrenched perspectives. That matters for education, deliberation, institutional design, and the interpretation of expert disagreement.

## Open questions

How sharply should the vault distinguish myside bias from confirmation bias? Which institutional settings reduce myside bias rather than merely rewarding more sophisticated defense of prior views? And should the bias be understood mainly as a cognitive limitation, a motivational distortion, or a predictable feature of argumentative social life?
