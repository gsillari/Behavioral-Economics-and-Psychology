# Availability, Affect, and Risk Perception

## Scope

This topic will gather the parts of behavioral economics that explain how salience, feeling, vivid examples, and fear shape judgments of risk and benefit. In the current vault, the main pages are [[Risk perception]], [[Availability heuristic]], [[Availability cascades]], [[Affect heuristic]], [[Risk as feelings]], [[Probability neglect]], [[Dread risk]], [[Disaster insurance]], and [[Emotions in decision-making]].

## Current focus

The topic page is currently a structural stub so [[Judgment, Belief, and Inference]] can link cleanly to its second-tier branches. A later drafting pass should connect the classic availability literature in [[Tversky and Kahneman 1973]] and [[Lichtenstein et al. 1978]] to the early risk-judgment framing in [[Slovic et al. 1982 - Facts versus Fears (in Kahneman et al. 1982, ch. 33)]], the broader psychometric framework of [[Slovic 1987]] and [[Risk perception]], and the affective-choice account in [[Loewenstein et al. 2007 - Risk as Feelings (in Loewenstein 2007, ch. 19)]], then outward to [[Risk as feelings]], crisis response, [[Probability neglect]], and public-policy relevance.
