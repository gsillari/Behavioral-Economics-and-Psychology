# Competing Views of Rationality

## Scope

This topic gathers the main rival answers to the question of what rationality is once the classical benchmark stops looking self-evident. In the current vault, the core pages are [[Bounded rationality]], [[Rationality wars]], [[Ecological rationality]], [[Evolutionary psychology]], and [[Argumentative theory of reasoning]].

The point of this branch is not only that people depart from formal norms. It is that researchers disagree about which norms matter, when they matter, and whether one framework should dominate the whole field. In historical terms, [[Simon 1955]] is the starting point of this branch in the current vault, because it asks how rationality should be modeled for agents with finite information and finite computational capacity. [[Wason 1968]] and [[Cosmides and Tooby 2000]] now add a compact experimental sequence: abstract conditional-reasoning failure, followed by an evolutionary reinterpretation of the same task when it is framed as social exchange.

## Current focus

The current topic skeleton is in place so that [[Foundations and Rationality]] can function as a hub of linked subtopics. The next full drafting pass should explain how [[Simon 1955]] and [[Bounded rationality]] broaden the critique of standard models, how [[Simon 1956]] begins to connect bounded rationality to environmental structure, how [[Simon 1979]] turns that critique into a mature account of organizational decision making and the scope of the economic sciences, how [[Wason selection task]] shows benchmark failure in conditional reasoning, how [[Cosmides and Tooby 2000]] turns that same task into evidence for domain-specific social reasoning, how [[Petracca 2017]] reopens the Simon branch by tying it to [[Cognitivism]] and to later disputes over [[Situated cognition]] and [[Embodied cognition]], how [[Ecological rationality]] challenges norm-first interpretations in a different register, how [[Rich 2016]] complicates that challenge by arguing that axiomatic and ecological standards may be best compared by the purposes they serve, how [[Mercier and Sperber 2011]] and [[Argumentative theory of reasoning]] shift attention to the social function of reasoning, and how [[Stanovich and West 2000]] makes the debate more empirically disciplined by stressing individual-differences evidence.
