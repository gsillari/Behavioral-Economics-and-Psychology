# Prediction and Models of Judgment

## Scope

This topic gathers the contrast between intuitive judgment and simple formal procedures in prediction and decision support. In the current vault, the key page is [[Clinical versus actuarial judgment]], with [[Dawes and Corrigan 1974]], [[Dawes 1979]], and [[Dawes et al. 1989]] providing the central linear-model and actuarial-judgment sequence. The branch is also supplemented by the blue-book units [[Kahneman and Tversky 1982 - Intuitive Prediction (in Kahneman et al. 1982, ch. 30)]] and [[Nisbett et al. 1982 - Improving Inductive Inference (in Kahneman et al. 1982, ch. 32)]].

## Current focus

The topic page is currently a structural stub so [[Judgment, Belief, and Inference]] can present its second-tier branches as links. Its core lesson is that predictive judgment is not only a matter of eliminating bias; it is also a matter of choosing decision procedures. [[Dawes and Corrigan 1974]] is especially useful here because it explains why simple linear models can perform well in structured prediction environments: the relevant cues are often directionally valid, measurement is noisy, and exact weights often matter less than stable cue combination. A later drafting pass should connect that linear-model branch to the forecasting and corrective-procedures side of the blue book, especially [[Kahneman and Tversky 1982 - Intuitive Prediction (in Kahneman et al. 1982, ch. 30)]] and [[Nisbett et al. 1982 - Improving Inductive Inference (in Kahneman et al. 1982, ch. 32)]]. The motivated-beliefs branch introduced by [[Motivated reasoning]] and sharpened by [[Myside bias]] is relevant nearby, but it is currently treated as a cross-cutting concept rather than as the core of this subtopic.
