# Representativeness and Statistical Error

## Scope

This topic will gather the parts of the judgment literature that center on similarity-based reasoning and the statistical errors that follow from it. In the current vault, the main pages are [[Representativeness heuristic]], [[Base-rate neglect]], [[Law of small numbers]], [[Conjunction fallacy]], and [[Overconfidence]], with [[Tversky and Kahneman 1983]] serving as the clearest formal journal-article anchor for the conjunction branch and [[Kahneman et al. 1982]] now giving the topic a richer internal map through its chapters on representativeness and base rates.

The base-rate branch is now especially strong. [[Ajzen 1977]] adds causal interpretation, [[Bar-Hillel 1980]] adds the relevance-and-specificity account, and [[Fischhoff and Bar-Hillel 1984]] adds the diagnosticity account. Together they show that base-rate neglect is not mere innumeracy. It depends on whether background statistical information feels causally relevant, specific to the case, or necessary because representativeness has failed to decide.
