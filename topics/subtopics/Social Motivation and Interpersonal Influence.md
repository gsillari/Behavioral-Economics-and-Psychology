# Social Motivation and Interpersonal Influence

## Scope

This topic gathers the parts of the vault where behavior is shaped by fairness, altruism, reciprocity, trust, social norms, self-serving interpretation, and interpersonal influence rather than by narrow self-interest alone. In the current vault, the key pages are [[Social preferences]], [[Fairness]], [[Altruism]], [[Trust]], [[Reciprocity]], [[Inequity aversion]], [[Betrayal aversion]], [[Cultural variation in social preferences]], [[Self-serving bias]], and [[Motivated reasoning]], with [[Rabin 1998]] serving as the broad psychology-and-economics source and [[Camerer 2003 - Chapter 02 - Dictator, Ultimatum, and Trust Games]] serving as the behavioral-game-theory synthesis for this branch.

The neighboring strategic branch now lives in [[Coordination, Convention, and Evolutionary Game Theory]]. That page handles coordination, convention, common knowledge, and Skyrms-style evolutionary dynamics. This split keeps the present subtopic focused on motives and interpersonal interpretation.

## Current focus

The topic now has an experimental-game entry path. [[Experimental games]] is the methodological hub. [[Ultimatum game]], [[Dictator game]], and [[Trust game]] introduce the settings through which fairness, giving, trust, reciprocity, and betrayal became economically visible. [[Public goods game]] adds the collective-action side: [[Free riding]] creates the standard problem, [[Conditional cooperation]] explains why cooperation can decay without universal selfishness, and [[Costly punishment]] shows how norm enforcement can stabilize contribution. [[Tragedy of the commons]] and [[Common-pool resources]] extend this branch toward open-access resources, environmental degradation, self-governance, and the institutional design of shared resources.

The internal conceptual split is between social motives and social interpretation. [[Altruism]], [[Inequity aversion]], and [[Reciprocity]] formalize three motivational routes: positive concern for another's welfare, concern with unequal outcomes, and response to perceived kindness or unkindness. [[Self-serving bias]] and [[Motivated reasoning]] show that people may also interpret fairness, entitlement, and responsibility in role-serving ways. [[Cultural variation in social preferences]] adds the anthropological correction: these motives are real, but their expression varies across societies and local social worlds.

A later drafting pass should connect this branch more fully to field settings: labor markets, bargaining, compliance, institutional legitimacy, and public policy.
