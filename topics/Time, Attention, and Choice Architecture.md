# Time, Attention, and Choice Architecture

This is **Topic 4 of 7** in the wiki's main topic sequence. The full order is: [[Foundations and Rationality]] -> [[Judgment, Belief, and Inference]] -> [[Choice, Preference, and Decision Under Risk]] -> [[Time, Attention, and Choice Architecture]] -> [[Social Behavior, Law, and Public Policy]] -> [[Ecological Rationality and Fast-and-Frugal Heuristics]] -> [[AI, Algorithms, and New Frontiers]].

## Scope

This topic gathers the parts of behavioral economics that focus on self-control, limited attention, context-dependent choice, and the institutional design responses those frictions invite. In the current vault, this is where dynamic inconsistency meets paternalism, and where empirical behavioral patterns become directly relevant for policy tools such as defaults, reminders, and incentive framing.

For PPE students, this topic matters because it links individual behavior to institutional design more directly than almost any other branch of the field. It matters for economics because it complicates intertemporal choice and welfare analysis. It matters for philosophy because it raises questions about autonomy, future selves, and paternalism. It matters for politics because many real policy interventions rely on exactly these mechanisms.

This branch therefore connects behavioral evidence to policy design in a more operational way than the earlier benchmark and judgment topics.

## Subtopics

### 4.1 [[Self-Control and Dynamic Inconsistency]]

This branch focuses on failures of intertemporal consistency and self-control. Its core pages are [[Time inconsistency]] and [[Projection bias]], with [[O'Donoghue and Rabin 1999]] supplying the central present-bias architecture for procrastination, preproperation, sophistication, and naivete.

### 4.2 [[Attention, Choice Context, and Decision Frictions]]

This branch focuses on inattention, overload, and friction in the decision environment. Its core pages are [[Limited attention]] and [[Menu effects]].

### 4.3 [[Welfare, Internalities, and Incentive Design]]

This branch focuses on welfare loss within the chooser and on incentive designs meant to respond to behavioral frictions. Its core pages are [[Internalities]] and [[Loss-framed incentives]].

### 4.4 [[Choice Architecture and Paternalism]]

This branch focuses on the justificatory and design debates around steering behavior without eliminating options. Its core pages are [[Libertarian paternalism]] and [[Asymmetric paternalism]].

## Links to the rest of the topic

The first branch explains why future-oriented choice is unstable. The second shows how attention and context make even present-oriented choice difficult. The third moves from those frictions to welfare interpretation and incentive design. The fourth turns to explicit institutional response. Together, they make this topic a bridge between behavioral evidence and behavioral public policy.

## Open questions

When should these frictions be interpreted as problems for welfare rather than ordinary features of constrained choice? How much of behavioral policy depends on self-control models rather than attention models? And where should a PPE course draw the line between helpful choice architecture and objectionable paternalism?
