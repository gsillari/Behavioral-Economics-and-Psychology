# Social Behavior, Law, and Public Policy

## Scope

This topic gathers the parts of behavioral economics that move beyond isolated individual choice and toward social motivation, institutions, legal design, public policy, and political decision-making more broadly. In the current vault, this is where other-regarding motives, interpersonal influence, welfare interpretation, institutional response, and now [[Behavioral political economy]] come together.

For PPE students, this topic is especially important because it sits at the intersection of all three letters. It matters for economics because it revises assumptions about motivation, law, and institutional response. It matters for philosophy because it raises questions about welfare, legitimacy, coercion, and fairness. It matters for politics because it concerns how institutions should react once behavioral findings are taken seriously.

This branch therefore turns the field outward: from how individuals choose to how institutions and political systems should understand, constrain, or guide those choices.

## Links to the rest of the topic

The first branch introduces the social side of behavior. The second asks how institutions and political actors should be understood once behavioral patterns matter for policy. The third places those questions in the longer legal and welfare framework of law and economics. Together, they give the vault a clearer route from descriptive behavioral findings to institutional evaluation.

## Open questions

How much of behavioral public policy depends on controversial assumptions about welfare? Should social preferences and interpersonal influence be treated as central to behavioral economics or as neighboring material? And how far can behavioral law and economics revise the classical legal-economic benchmark before it becomes a different framework altogether?
